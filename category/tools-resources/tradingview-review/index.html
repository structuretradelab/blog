What is TradingView?

TradingView is a cloud-based charting platform. Unlike MT4/MT5, which you install on your computer, TradingView runs in your browser. Your charts, indicators, and drawings are saved in the cloud — accessible from any device.

Key facts:

  • Founded in 2011, now the most popular charting platform worldwide
  • Free tier available with basic features
  • Paid tiers from $13-$200/month (or cheaper with annual billing)
  • Integrates with many brokers for direct trading
📊 What it's best for: Technical analysis, charting, drawing tools, indicators, and sharing ideas with the community.

Key Features for Forex Traders

Charting Tools

  • Advanced drawing tools: Trend lines, channels, Fibonacci, pitchforks, and more
  • 100+ indicators: RSI, MACD, moving averages, Bollinger Bands, and custom scripts
  • Multiple chart layouts: View up to 8 charts simultaneously
  • Multiple timeframes: From 1-second to monthly
  • Pine Script: Create your own indicators and strategies

Market Data

  • Forex: All major, minor, and exotic pairs
  • Cryptocurrency: Bitcoin, Ethereum, and hundreds of altcoins
  • Stocks, indices, commodities, futures
  • Economic calendar built-in

Community & Social

  • Publishing ideas: Share your analysis with millions of traders
  • Script library: Thousands of community-built indicators
  • Chat rooms: Discuss markets in real-time
📌 My Favorite Feature: The cloud saving. I can draw support/resistance levels on my desktop, then open the same chart on my phone later. Everything syncs automatically.

Pros and Cons

✅ Pros

  • Beautiful interface: Clean, modern, customizable
  • Cloud-based: Access charts from anywhere
  • Huge community: Millions of traders share ideas
  • Powerful drawing tools: Best in class for technical analysis
  • Pine Script: Create custom indicators without programming
  • Mobile app: Excellent iOS and Android apps

❌ Cons

  • Not for execution (on most brokers): You need to switch to MT4/MT5 to trade
  • Data delays on free tier: Real-time data requires paid subscription for some markets
  • Expensive for full features: Premium tiers add up quickly
  • Requires internet: No offline mode (unlike MT4)
  • Learning curve: Lots of features can overwhelm beginners
⚠️ Important: TradingView is for analysis, not execution. Most traders analyze on TradingView, then execute on MT4/MT5. Don't expect to place complex orders (trailing stops, OCO, etc.) on TradingView.

My Verdict

For beginners: ✅ Yes, use the free version. It's excellent for learning technical analysis. The drawing tools and clean interface make it much easier to learn than MT4's dated interface.

For intermediate traders: ✅ Yes, consider Pro or Pro+. If you need more charts, alerts, or indicators, the paid tiers are worth it. Get the annual plan to save money.

For advanced traders: ⚠️ It depends. If you need complex order types or algorithmic trading, stick with MT4/MT5. Use TradingView for analysis only.

⚠️ My Advice: Don't buy a paid subscription until you've used the free version for at least 3 months. Most beginners never need the paid features. Save your money for your trading account.
