But if you're new to it, the interface can feel overwhelming. There are hundreds of indicators, dozens of drawing tools, and more settings than you know what to do with.

In this guide, let's walk through how to use TradingView for forex analysis — the essential setup, the tools that matter, and how to keep your charts clean and actionable.

Getting Started: Your First Chart

If you don't have a TradingView account yet, sign up for the free version. It's more than enough for forex analysis. The free plan gives you one chart per tab, three indicators, and one alert .

Step 1: Open a Forex Pair

Use the symbol search bar at the top left. Type "EURUSD" or "FX:EURUSD" and select the pair you want to analyze. TradingView will load the chart instantly.

Step 2: Choose Your Timeframe

The timeframe selector is at the top of the chart. For forex analysis, start with the Daily (D) or 4-Hour (4H) chart. These higher timeframes filter out noise and show the true trend.

Step 3: Select Candlestick Chart

TradingView defaults to candlesticks, which is what most forex traders use. If it's not selected, click the chart type icon and choose "Candles".

📌 Pro Tip: Save your chart layout. Once you've set up your colors, indicators, and drawing tools, click the "Save" button (or press Ctrl+S). TradingView will remember your setup for next time.

The Essential Chart Setup

A clean chart is an effective chart. Here's how I set up mine for forex analysis:

Colors and Contrast

Use a simple, high-contrast color scheme. I prefer a dark background with white/black candles, or a light background with green/red candles. Avoid bright, distracting colors.

Grid and Sessions

Turn the grid to "light" or off entirely. If you trade specific sessions (London/New York), you can highlight them using the Sessions tool in the chart settings.

The 200 EMA Baseline

Add a 200-period Exponential Moving Average (EMA) to your chart. This is your trend filter. Price above the 200 EMA = uptrend bias. Price below = downtrend bias.

⚠️ Don't Overcrowd: If your chart looks like a rainbow of lines and colors, you've added too much. Strip it back to price, the 200 EMA, and your key levels. That's it.

Drawing Tools: Marking Key Levels

The drawing tools are on the left side of the chart. These are the ones you actually need for forex analysis:

Tool What It's For How to Use It
Horizontal Line Marking support and resistance Click the level, drag to the key price zone. Use zones, not exact lines.
Trend Line Connecting swing highs or lows Draw through the wicks, not the bodies. Wait for 3 touches to confirm.
Fibonacci Retracement Finding pullback zones Draw from swing low to swing high (uptrend). Watch the 38.2%, 50%, 61.8% levels.
Rectangle Marking supply/demand zones Draw around consolidation areas or strong reversal zones.

My process: I mark the major daily levels first, then drop to the 4-hour to refine them. I use rectangles for zones, not single lines. This gives price room to breathe without triggering false signals.

Indicators: Keep It Simple

TradingView has hundreds of built-in indicators and thousands of community scripts . You don't need most of them.

The three indicators I recommend for forex analysis:

  1. 200 EMA (Trend Filter): As mentioned above. Tells you the direction. Don't trade against it.
  2. RSI (14): Use it for divergence, not overbought/oversold signals in trends. Look for RSI making lower highs while price makes higher highs (bearish divergence).
  3. ATR (14): Use it to measure volatility. If ATR is high, widen your stops. If low, tighten them. Don't set stops at a fixed pip distance without checking ATR.
📌 Pro Tip: To add an indicator, click the "Indicators" button at the top, search for the name, and click it. To adjust settings, hover over the indicator name on the chart and click the gear icon.

Alerts: Let the Platform Watch for You

One of TradingView's best features is the alert system. You don't need to stare at charts all day. Set an alert at your key level, and TradingView will notify you when price arrives.

How to Set a Price Alert

  1. Click the "Alert" button at the top of the chart (or press Alt+A)
  2. Set the condition: "Crossing" or "Crossing Up/Down"
  3. Enter the price level you want to be notified about
  4. Choose your notification method (email, pop-up, mobile app)
  5. Click "Create"

My alert strategy: I set alerts at my key support/resistance levels on the 4-hour chart. When the alert fires, I check the chart for a candlestick confirmation. If it's there, I switch to my broker platform and execute. If not, I reset the alert and wait.

⚠️ Free Plan Limitation: The free plan allows only one active alert at a time. If you need more, you'll need a paid plan. For most beginners, one alert is enough — focus on one setup at a time .

My Forex Analysis Workflow

Here's exactly how I use TradingView for forex analysis every day:

  1. Start with the Daily chart: Check the 200 EMA. Is price above (bullish) or below (bearish)? Mark the major support/resistance zones.
  2. Drop to the 4-Hour chart: Refine the levels. Look for a setup forming (pullback to a level, consolidation, etc.).
  3. Check RSI and ATR: Is there divergence? What's the current volatility? This informs my stop distance.
  4. Set an alert: If price isn't at my level yet, set an alert and walk away.
  5. When the alert fires: Check the 1-hour chart for a candlestick confirmation (pin bar, engulfing).
  6. Execute on MT4/MT5: TradingView is for analysis. I place the actual trade on my broker's platform.
📌 The Key Mindset: TradingView is a tool for finding setups, not for forcing trades. If there's no setup, close the tab and come back tomorrow. The platform will still be there.
