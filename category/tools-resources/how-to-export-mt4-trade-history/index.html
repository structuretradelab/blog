You've been trading for a few months. You want to analyze your performance. Or maybe it's tax season, and you need to report your trades.

The problem: your trade history is buried inside MT4, and you don't know how to get it out.

Exporting your MT4 trade history to Excel takes about 30 seconds once you know where to look. In this guide, let's walk through how to do it — and what to do with the data once you have it.

💡 Key Takeaway: Your MT4 trade history is in the Terminal window under the Account History tab. Right-click, select Save as Report, and choose .xlsx or .html format.

Why Export Your Trade History?

Your trade history is your most valuable data. Exporting it lets you:

  • Journal properly: Analyze every trade outside the platform — entries, exits, risk, outcome
  • Identify patterns: See which setups work and which fail over hundreds of trades
  • Calculate performance: Win rate, average risk reward, drawdown, expectancy
  • Prepare taxes: Most jurisdictions require trade records for reporting
  • Back up your data: If you switch brokers or platforms, you keep your history
📌 Pro Tip: Export your history at the end of every month. Save it in a dedicated folder. After a year, you'll have 12 files that tell the complete story of your trading.

Step-by-Step: Exporting from MT4

Here's the exact process :

Step 1: Open the Terminal Window

In MT4, press Ctrl + T or click View → Terminal at the bottom of the platform. The Terminal panel will open at the bottom of your screen.

Step 2: Go to the Account History Tab

At the bottom of the Terminal panel, click the Account History tab. This shows all your closed trades.

Step 3: Select the Date Range

Right-click anywhere in the Account History area. Select All History to see every trade, or Custom Period to choose specific dates.

Step 4: Save as Report

Right-click again and select Save as Report.

Step 5: Choose Your Format

A dialog box appears. You have two main options:

  • .html — A web page report. Opens in any browser. Can be opened in Excel but formatting may be messy.
  • .xlsx — A native Excel file. Opens directly in Excel with clean columns.

Choose .xlsx if you want clean data for analysis. Choose .html if you want a formatted report you can print or share.

Step 6: Save the File

Name the file with the date range (e.g., `MT4_History_2026-11.xlsx`) and save it to a folder you'll remember.

📌 Pro Tip: Some versions of MT4 also offer a .csv option. If you're comfortable with data analysis, CSV is the cleanest format for importing into Excel, Google Sheets, or Python.

HTML vs XLSX: Which Format to Choose

Format Best For Pros Cons
.xlsx Data analysis, journaling Clean columns, opens directly in Excel, easy to sort/filter Some MT4 builds may not offer this option
.html Printing, sharing, visual review Formatted report, includes summary statistics Data is embedded in HTML — harder to manipulate
.csv Advanced analysis, coding Universal format, works with any tool No formatting, requires manual column mapping
⚠️ If .xlsx isn't available: Save as .html, then open the file in Excel. Excel can read HTML tables. The formatting won't be perfect, but the data will be there.

What's missing: Your stop loss and take profit levels aren't always included in the basic export. If you need those, you'll need to record them manually in your journal.

How to Use the Data for Analysis

Once you have the Excel file, here's what to do with it:

1. Calculate Your Core Metrics

Add these columns/formulas to your spreadsheet:

  • Win Rate: Count of winning trades ÷ total trades
  • Average Win: Average profit of winning trades
  • Average Loss: Average loss of losing trades
  • Profit Factor: Total profit ÷ total loss
  • Expectancy: (Win Rate × Average Win) − (Loss Rate × Average Loss)

2. Group Trades by Setup

Add a column for "Setup Type" (e.g., "Support Bounce," "Breakout," "Pin Bar"). Tag each trade. Then use Excel's PivotTable to see which setups have the highest win rate.

3. Group Trades by Session

Add a column for "Session" (London, New York, Asian). See which session produces your best results.

4. Group Trades by Day of Week

See if you perform better on certain days. Some traders are consistently profitable on Tuesday-Thursday but lose on Mondays and Fridays.

📌 Pro Tip: After 50+ trades, patterns emerge. You might discover that your "Breakout" setup has a 25% win rate, while your "Support Bounce" setup has 60%. That's actionable information you can't get from memory.

Troubleshooting Common Issues

Problem Solution
Account History is empty Right-click in the Account History tab and select "All History" or "Last 3 Months." MT4 only shows recent trades by default.
No "Save as Report" option Make sure you're right-clicking inside the Account History list, not on a specific trade. Try right-clicking on a blank area.
Excel file won't open If you saved as .html, change the file extension to .xls and try again. Or open Excel first, then File → Open and select the .html file.
Missing trades Check that your date range includes all trades. Some brokers split history across multiple server connections.
Formatting is messy in Excel This happens with .html files. Use Text to Columns (Data → Text to Columns) to split the data into proper columns.
⚠️ Important: Your MT4 history is stored on your computer. If you uninstall MT4 or switch computers, you lose it. Export regularly and back up your files.

Your History is Your Edge

Most traders never export their history. They rely on memory. Memory is biased — it remembers the wins and forgets the losses. The spreadsheet doesn't.

Open the Terminal. Go to Account History. Right-click and Save as Report. Choose .xlsx. Save it with the date. Do that every month, and you'll build a data asset that makes you a better trader.
