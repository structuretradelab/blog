If you're running an Expert Advisor (EA), copy trading, or any automated strategy, a VPS is not a luxury — it's infrastructure. It keeps your platform running 24/5, even when your computer is off or your internet drops.

But with hundreds of VPS providers claiming "ultra-low latency" and "1ms to broker," how do you choose? Most of the marketing is misleading or irrelevant for retail traders.

In this guide, let's cut through the noise and walk through what actually matters when choosing a VPS for forex trading.

💡 Key Takeaway: Location matters more than specs. A modest VPS in the same data center as your broker will beat a powerful VPS in the wrong city every time. Choose proximity first, then specs.

What a VPS Actually Does (and Doesn't Do)

A VPS (Virtual Private Server) is a remote computer in a data center that runs your trading platform 24/5. It solves two problems:

  • Uptime: Your platform stays connected even when your home power or internet fails
  • Latency: If the VPS is near your broker's servers, order execution is faster

What a VPS does NOT do:

  • It won't make a bad EA profitable
  • It won't fix broker-side issues like requotes or widened spreads
  • It won't replace proper strategy testing
⚠️ Important: A VPS speeds up execution. It does not improve your strategy. If your EA has a negative expectancy, a VPS just helps it lose money faster.

Location: The Most Important Factor

This is the single biggest mistake new VPS users make: hosting the VPS near themselves instead of near their broker .

Your trading software makes thousands of requests to your broker's server. If your VPS is in the same data center as the broker, latency drops from typical home figures (50-200ms) to under 5ms .

Major Data Centers for Forex

Data Center Location Best For
Equinix LD4 London, UK European brokers, London session liquidity
Equinix NY4 New York, USA US brokers, New York session
Equinix TY3 Tokyo, Japan JPY pairs, Asian session

How to find your broker's location: Check your MT4/MT5 terminal. Go to Tools → Options → Server or right-click your account in the Navigator to see your assigned server . Then ask your broker's support which data center they use.

📌 Pro Tip: The latency from your home to the VPS is irrelevant. The only path that matters is VPS to broker .

What Specs Do You Actually Need?

MetaTrader is not a heavy application. You don't need a gaming PC. Here's what's realistic :

Setup RAM vCPU Disk
1 MT4/MT5, 1-3 EAs 1-2 GB 1-2 vCPU 20-40 GB SSD
2 instances, 5-10 EAs 2-4 GB 2 vCPU 40-60 GB SSD
4+ instances, complex portfolios 4-8 GB 2-4 vCPU 60-80 GB SSD

What to prioritize:

  • Dedicated vCPU (not burst/shared) — shared CPU throttles during news spikes, exactly when you need it most
  • 99.99% uptime SLA — not 99.9%
  • NVMe or SSD storage — SATA SSD is fine; you don't need NVMe
⚠️ Avoid: Budget plans with "burst" CPU. They throttle under load — which means they fail during NFP, FOMC, and other high-volatility events .

How Much Should You Pay?

Reasonable forex VPS plans sit between $15 and $50 per month for a single MetaTrader instance .

  • Under $10/month: Usually oversubscribed shared hardware. Expect instability during news
  • $15-$35/month: The sweet spot for most retail traders
  • $40-$80/month: Higher specs, multiple instances, or premium colocation

Free Broker-Provided VPS

Many brokers offer a free VPS to clients who meet a monthly trading volume requirement (often 10+ lots). If you qualify, this is often the best option because:

  • The VPS is hosted in the same data center as your broker's servers — the lowest possible latency
  • It's free (assuming you'd be trading that volume anyway)
  • Setup is handled by the broker — no server configuration required

Questions to ask your broker before accepting:

  • What is the minimum monthly volume required?
  • What specs does the VPS have (RAM, vCPU, dedicated or shared)?
  • Are there restrictions on which EAs or scripts I can run?
  • What happens if I don't meet the volume requirement in a given month?
📌 Pro Tip: Even if you don't qualify for a free broker VPS, ask your broker which data center their servers are in. That information lets you choose a third-party VPS in the same location — which is what actually matters.

What to Look for in a Provider

Rather than recommending specific providers, here's a checklist to evaluate any forex VPS service:

Feature What to Look For Red Flag
Data Center Same location as your broker (LD4, NY4, TY3, etc.) Only one location available, or vague about where servers are
CPU Dedicated vCPU, clearly stated "Burst" or "shared" without disclosure
Uptime SLA 99.99% with a clear refund policy No SLA, or "best effort" uptime

⚠️ Always test first: Most providers offer a trial or money-back period. Run your EA on a demo account for at least two weeks before committing to a long-term plan .

Common VPS Mistakes

1. Choosing location based on where you live

The VPS should be near your broker, not near you. Your home connection to the VPS only matters when you log in to check on it .

2. Buying too much RAM

A single MT4 instance with a few EAs runs fine on 1-2 GB. You don't need 16 GB for one chart .

3. Using a shared/burst CPU plan

Burst plans throttle during high load — exactly when news hits. Pay for dedicated vCPU .

4. Forgetting Windows updates

Automatic Windows reboots can take your EA offline mid-session. Set updates to "notify only" or schedule a fixed weekly reboot outside market hours .

5. No kill switch

If you lose access to the VPS, you need a documented way to close positions. Usually, this means calling your broker's dealing desk .

Location First, Specs Second, Price Third

Choosing a VPS is not about finding the most powerful server. It's about finding the right server for your broker.

Find your broker's data center. Choose a VPS in that location. Get 2 GB RAM and 1-2 dedicated vCPU. Pay $15-35/month. Test on demo for two weeks. That's how your VPS does exactly what it's supposed to do — keep your EA running reliably.
