There are hundreds of forex news sites. Most of them are noise. A few are genuinely useful. And the "best" one depends entirely on how you trade.

A scalper needs real-time headlines. A swing trader needs context and analysis. A beginner needs education, not a firehose of data. Choosing the wrong source for your style wastes time and adds stress without improving results.

In this guide, let's walk through the most reliable forex news sources, what each is actually good for, and how to build a simple news routine that doesn't overwhelm you.

💡 Key Takeaway: You don't need ten news sources. Pick two: one for scheduled events (an economic calendar) and one for context or breaking news. Everything else is optional.

Why News Matters (But Not How You Think)

News moves markets. That's not debatable. A surprise rate hike, a shock CPI print, or an unexpected geopolitical event can move a currency pair hundreds of pips in minutes.

But here's what most beginners get wrong: you don't need to trade the news to benefit from it.

The primary value of a news source is risk management. Knowing when high-impact events are scheduled tells you when to reduce position size, tighten stops, or simply stay out of the market. The secondary value is context — understanding why a currency is moving, not just that it's moving.

⚠️ The Hard Truth: Retail traders have no edge trading news. By the time you read a headline and click, algorithms have already reacted. Use news to avoid surprises, not to chase moves.

How to Filter the Noise

The biggest problem with news isn't a lack of information — it's too much. Here's how to filter it:

  1. Ignore low-impact events. If it's not a central bank decision, inflation data, or employment report, it probably doesn't matter for your pair.
  2. Focus on the currencies you trade. If you trade EUR/USD, you don't need to read every headline about the Australian dollar.
  3. Set alerts, don't scroll. Use the economic calendar to set notifications for high-impact events. Don't refresh news sites all day.
  4. Understand the relationship, not the headline. USD pairs react to Fed policy. Commodity currencies react to oil and gold. Learn these links instead of trying to track everything.
⚠️ Avoid social media as a primary source. Twitter/X and Telegram groups are full of fake news, pump-and-dump schemes, and unverified claims. If you use them, follow only official central bank and reputable analyst accounts.

My Simple News Routine

Here's what I actually do every day. It takes 10 minutes:

  1. Morning: Open ForexFactory. Check the calendar for red events in the next 24 hours. Note the times.
  2. Before trading: If a red event is within 30 minutes, I don't open new trades. If I have open trades, I tighten stops or close.
  3. During the session: I don't watch news. I watch my alerts. If an alert fires, I check the chart for a setup.
  4. After the release: If something big happened, I check Reuters or Bloomberg for a one-paragraph summary to understand the context.
  5. Weekly: I read one DailyFX or FXStreet analysis piece to understand the bigger picture for the pairs I trade.
📌 Pro Tip: The best news source is the one you actually check consistently. A simple ForexFactory alert you act on is worth more than a Bloomberg terminal you ignore.

The Trap: Trading the News vs. Knowing the News

This is the most important distinction in this entire guide.

Knowing the news means you're aware of scheduled events, you understand the macro context, and you adjust your risk accordingly. This is essential.

Trading the news means you try to profit from the spike itself. This is gambling for retail traders. Spreads widen dramatically, price gaps, and by the time you react, the move is often over.

⚠️ The Bottom Line: Use news to protect yourself, not to gamble. The calendar tells you when to be careful. It doesn't tell you which direction to trade.

Two Sources, One Routine

You don't need to read everything. You need two sources: a calendar for scheduled events, and a reliable wire service for context. That's it.

Pick ForexFactory for events. Pick Reuters or Bloomberg for context. Check them for 10 minutes each morning. Avoid trading around red events. That's how you stop getting blindsided by news — without drowning in it.
