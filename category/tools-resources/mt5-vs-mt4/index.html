MT4 Overview: The Industry Standard

MetaTrader 4 (MT4) was released in 2005. Despite being 20+ years old, it remains the most popular forex trading platform in the world.

Why traders still love MT4:

  • Simple and stable: It rarely crashes. It's been tested by millions of traders.
  • Massive ecosystem: Thousands of indicators and Expert Advisors (EAs) are available.
  • Universal broker support: Almost every forex broker offers MT4.
  • Low learning curve: You can learn the basics in a few hours.
  • Lightweight: Runs smoothly even on older computers.

What MT4 lacks:

  • No built-in economic calendar
  • Limited to forex (no stocks, futures, or crypto)
  • Fewer order types than MT5
  • Only 9 timeframes
📌 Best for: Forex-only traders, beginners, EA users, anyone who wants a simple, reliable platform.

MT5 Overview: The Modern Upgrade

MetaTrader 5 (MT5) was released in 2010 as the successor to MT4. It's more powerful and versatile, but also more complex.

What MT5 does better:

  • More timeframes: 21 timeframes vs 9 on MT4
  • More order types: 6 pending orders vs 4 on MT4
  • Built-in economic calendar: No plugins needed
  • Multi-asset support: Trade stocks, futures, and crypto, not just forex
  • More indicators: 38 built-in vs 30 on MT4
  • Depth of Market (DOM): See market depth for stocks/futures

What MT5 lacks:

  • Smaller ecosystem: Fewer EAs and indicators available
  • Less broker support: Many forex brokers still don't offer MT5
  • Steeper learning curve: More features = more complexity
  • EA compatibility: MT4 EAs don't work on MT5 (different code language)
📌 Best for: Traders who trade multiple asset classes (forex + stocks + futures), advanced order types, or need the economic calendar built-in.

Key Differences in Detail

Order Types

MT5 adds Buy Stop Limit and Sell Stop Limit — orders that combine stop and limit features. Most retail traders never use these. For simple market and pending orders, both platforms work fine.

Timeframes

MT4 has 9 timeframes (M1, M5, M15, M30, H1, H4, D1, W1, MN). MT5 adds M2, M3, M4, M6, M10, M12, H2, H3, H6, H8, H12. Most traders use M5, M15, H1, H4, D1. The extra timeframes are rarely needed.

Economic Calendar

MT5 has a built-in economic calendar. MT4 requires a third-party plugin. If you want news alerts directly in your platform, MT5 has an advantage.

Multi-Asset Trading

MT4 is forex-only. MT5 supports forex, stocks, indices, commodities, futures, and crypto. If you only trade forex, this doesn't matter. If you trade other markets, MT5 is better.

Ecosystem & Community

MT4 has been around for 20+ years. There are millions of traders, thousands of EAs, and endless tutorials. MT5's ecosystem is smaller. If you want to use Expert Advisors, MT4 has far more options.

⚠️ Important: MT4 EAs do NOT work on MT5. The programming language (MQL4 vs MQL5) is different. If you have existing EAs, stick with MT4.

Which One Should You Choose?

Choose MT4 if:

  • You only trade forex
  • You're a beginner (simpler is better)
  • You use Expert Advisors (EAs) — vast library available
  • Your broker only offers MT4 (most brokers offer MT4)
  • You want the platform with the most educational resources

Choose MT5 if:

  • You trade multiple asset classes (stocks, futures, crypto)
  • You need advanced order types (stop limit orders)
  • You want a built-in economic calendar
  • You trade on higher timeframes (need more granular options)
  • Your broker only offers MT5 (less common, but some brokers do)
📌 My Recommendation: Start with MT4. It's simpler, has more resources, and is supported by virtually every broker. Once you're consistently profitable and want to trade other markets, consider switching to MT5.

My Verdict

I've used both platforms. I still use MT4 daily. Not because MT5 is bad — because MT4 does everything I need, and I don't trade stocks or futures.

Most beginners should start with MT4. It's simpler, more stable, and has far more educational resources. You're not missing anything by choosing MT4.

If you outgrow MT4 — if you start trading stocks, need advanced orders, or want the built-in calendar — then consider MT5. But you'll know when that time comes.

⚠️ Bottom Line: Don't overthink this. Both platforms can execute trades. Both have charts and indicators. Pick one, learn it, and focus on your trading strategy. The platform won't make you profitable. Your risk management and discipline will.
