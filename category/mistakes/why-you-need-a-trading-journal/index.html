Why You Need a Trading Journal

  • Identifies patterns: You'll see exactly which setups work and which fail.
  • Reveals emotional mistakes: "Moved stop loss out of fear" becomes visible when you write it down.
  • Tracks progress: You can see your win rate, average risk reward, and drawdown over time.
  • Builds discipline: Knowing you'll have to log a rule break makes you less likely to break it.
  • Accelerates learning: One journaled trade teaches more than ten unjournaled trades.
⚠️ The Hard Truth: If you're not journaling, you're not serious about improving. Every professional trader I know keeps a detailed journal.

What to Log in Your Trading Journal

Here's what I log for every trade. You can start with these fields and adjust as needed.

Trade Information (The Data)

  • Date and time: When you entered and exited
  • Currency pair: EUR/USD, GBP/USD, etc.
  • Direction: Long or short
  • Entry price: Where you entered
  • Stop loss price: Where your stop was placed
  • Take profit price: Where your target was
  • Position size (lots): How many lots you traded
  • Risk amount ($): Your dollar risk
  • Profit/loss ($ and pips): The outcome
  • Risk reward ratio: Reward ÷ Risk

Trade Reasoning (The Why)

  • Setup name: "Support bounce," "Moving average crossover," etc.
  • Reason for trade: "Price bounced off 50 EMA" — not "felt right."

Psychology (The Mindset)

  • Emotional state before entry: Calm, anxious, overconfident?
  • Did you follow your plan? Yes or no. If no, what rule did you break?
  • Lesson learned: What will you do differently next time?
📌 Pro Tip: The psychology section is the most important. The numbers tell you what happened. The psychology tells you why.

How to Analyze Your Journal

Journaling is useless if you don't review it. Set aside time every week to analyze.

Weekly Review Questions

  • What was my win rate this week? Is it in line with my strategy?
  • What was my average risk reward? Am I maintaining 1:2 minimum?
  • Which setups worked? Double down on what's working.
  • Which setups failed? Consider removing or adjusting them.
  • How many times did I break my rules? What triggered it?
  • What was my emotional state before losing trades? Tired? Angry? Overconfident?
⚠️ The Key Insight: Patterns emerge when you look at 20-50 trades. One trade tells you nothing. Fifty trades tell you everything.

How to Start Today

  1. Choose your tool: Google Sheets, Excel, Notion, or a dedicated journaling app.
  2. Copy the template: Use the link above to get the free template.
  3. Commit to one week: Journal every trade for 7 days. See how it feels.
  4. Schedule review time: Every Sunday, spend 15 minutes reviewing your week.
  5. Adjust as needed: Add columns that matter to you. Remove ones that don't.
📌 Start Simple: Don't overcomplicate. Even a notebook with five columns is better than nothing. Start with: Date, Trade (win/loss), Emotion, Did I follow my plan?, Lesson. Add more later.
