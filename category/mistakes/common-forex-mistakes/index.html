1. Trading Without a Plan

The mistake: Opening trades based on "feeling" or "hunch" without clear entry, exit, or risk management rules.

Why it's dangerous: Without a plan, every decision is emotional. You'll hold losers too long and cut winners too short.

How to avoid it: Write down your trading plan. Include: which pairs you trade, entry rules, exit rules, stop loss placement, risk per trade. Review it before every single trade.

⚠️ The Rule: If you can't write down your trade plan in 2-3 sentences before entering, you shouldn't take the trade.

2. Using Too Much Leverage

The mistake: Trading with 1:100, 1:500, or even 1:1000 leverage as a beginner.

Why it's dangerous: High leverage amplifies losses. A small move against you can wipe out your entire account.

How to avoid it: Use 1:10 or 1:20 leverage for your first year. You don't need more. Anyone offering 1:1000 is preying on beginners.

3. Trading Without a Stop Loss

The mistake: Entering a trade without a predetermined stop loss, hoping the market will turn.

Why it's dangerous: One bad trade without a stop loss can wipe out weeks or months of profits.

How to avoid it: Never enter a trade without a stop loss. Place it before you hit "buy" or "sell." Never move it further away.

📖 Related Reading: Stop Loss Explained — Master stop loss placement.

4. Revenge Trading

The mistake: Taking a loss, getting angry, and immediately opening another trade to "win back" what you lost — often with larger size.

Why it's dangerous: Revenge trading turns a small loss into a catastrophic loss.

How to avoid it: Set a rule: after any loss, step away for 15 minutes. After two consecutive losses, stop trading for the day.

📖 Related Reading: Revenge Trading: What It Is and How to Stop Forever — Break the cycle.

5. Overtrading

The mistake: Taking too many trades — often because of boredom, impatience, or the need to "do something."

Why it's dangerous: Overtrading increases transaction costs, reduces trade quality, and leads to emotional burnout.

How to avoid it: Set a daily/weekly trade limit. Focus on quality setups, not quantity. Sometimes the best trade is no trade.

📖 Related Reading: Overtrading: Why Less Trades Often Mean More Profit — Learn to stop overtrading.

6. Moving Stop Losses

The mistake: Moving your stop loss further away when the market approaches it, hoping it will reverse.

Why it's dangerous: It turns a planned small loss into a catastrophic large loss. It also destroys your risk-reward ratio.

How to avoid it: Never move your stop loss further away. Ever. If you're tempted, close the trade and walk away.

⚠️ The Hard Truth: Every trader who has blown an account has moved a stop loss at least once. Don't be one of them.

7. Following Social Media Gurus

The mistake: Copying trades or buying courses from Instagram/Facebook/TikTok "gurus" who promise guaranteed profits.

Why it's dangerous: 99% of them are faking it. They make money selling courses, not trading.

How to avoid it: If someone promises guaranteed profits, run. If they sell a course instead of sharing free value, be skeptical. Learn to trade yourself.

8. Skipping the Demo Account

The mistake: Going straight to a live account without practicing on a demo.

Why it's dangerous: You're learning with real money. Every mistake costs you. Most beginners blow their first live account within weeks.

How to avoid it: Spend at least 2-3 months on a demo account. Prove you can be profitable before risking real money.

📖 Related Reading: Demo Trading Explained — The complete case for demo trading.

9. Risking Too Much Per Trade

The mistake: Risking 5%, 10%, or even more of your account on a single trade.

Why it's dangerous: A few losses in a row will cripple or destroy your account.

How to avoid it: Risk 1-2% maximum per trade. This is non-negotiable for long-term survival.

📖 Related Reading: Risk Management 101 — Master the 1% rule.

10. Not Keeping a Trading Journal

The mistake: Trading without recording your trades, emotions, and lessons learned.

Why it's dangerous: You can't improve what you don't measure. Without a journal, you repeat the same mistakes.

How to avoid it: Start a trading journal today. Track every trade — entry, exit, position size, emotional state, outcome, lesson learned.
