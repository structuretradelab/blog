What is the 1% Rule?

The 1% rule is simple: never risk more than 1% of your account balance on any single trade.

How it works:

  • If you have a $10,000 account, your maximum loss per trade is $100
  • If you have a $1,000 account, your maximum loss per trade is $10
  • If you have a $100 account, your maximum loss per trade is $1

Your risk amount is determined by your stop loss distance × position size. You adjust your position size so that your stop loss never exceeds 1% of your account.

📌 The Golden Rule: Risk 1% per trade. Always. No exceptions. This is the single most important rule in trading.

Why 1% is the Golden Standard

Most successful traders risk 1-2% per trade. Here's why 1% is the industry standard:

  • Survival: You can survive a losing streak without blowing your account
  • Emotional control: Small losses don't trigger fear or revenge trading
  • Consistency: The same risk every trade builds discipline
  • Compounding: Small consistent gains add up over time
  • Recovery: A 10% drawdown requires 11% gain to recover — manageable with 1% risk

Professional traders don't use the 1% rule because they're scared. They use it because they've survived long enough to know it works.

⚠️ The Hard Truth: Most beginners blow their accounts because they risk too much. The 1% rule is the difference between surviving and blowing up.

How to Apply the 1% Rule

Here's exactly how to apply the 1% rule to your trading:

Step 1: Calculate Your Risk Amount

Risk Amount = Account Balance × 1%

Example: $5,000 × 0.01 = $50

Step 2: Determine Your Stop Loss Distance

How many pips is your stop loss from your entry? Example: 20 pips

Step 3: Calculate Your Position Size

Position Size = Risk Amount ÷ (Stop Loss in Pips × Pip Value)

Example: $50 ÷ (20 × $0.10) = 0.25 lots

Recommended way: Use the position size calculator to do this instantly. It removes manual errors.

⚠️ Important: Your stop loss and position size work together. If your stop is wider, reduce your position size. If your stop is tighter, you can increase your position size. Your risk per trade remains 1%.

Common Excuses for Breaking the Rule

Here's what you'll tell yourself when you want to break the 1% rule — and why you shouldn't:

Excuse 1: "I'm confident about this trade."

Every trader is confident before every trade. Confidence doesn't change probability. Risk 1% like every other trade.

Excuse 2: "It's a small account. I need to risk more to grow."

A small account is more fragile, not less. Risking 5% on a $100 account means one loss wipes out 5% — you can't recover from a losing streak.

Excuse 3: "I've been on a winning streak."

Winning streaks don't guarantee future wins. In fact, overconfidence after wins is one of the most dangerous psychological states.

Excuse 4: "I'll just do it this one time."

One time becomes two. Two becomes a habit. Habits become account-blowing patterns. Stick to the rule.

⚠️ The Bottom Line: Every trader who broke the 1% rule had a "good reason." And most of them are no longer trading. The rule is not optional.

The Advanced Version: Fixed Fractional

Fixed fractional position sizing takes the 1% rule one step further: you risk 1% of your current account balance on every trade.

How it works:

  • Account grows to $11,000 → risk $110 per trade
  • Account drops to $9,000 → risk $90 per trade

Why it's better:

  • Compounds your wins (risk more when you're winning)
  • Protects during drawdowns (risk less when you're losing)
  • Automatically adjusts to your account size
📌 Pro Tip: Use fixed fractional position sizing. Update your position size weekly based on your current account balance. This compounds your growth and protects your capital.
