Maximum Drawdown: What It Is and How to Control It

📅 Published: May 2026 📖 7 min read 🏷️ Category: Risk Management

Here's a number that every trader should know: their maximum drawdown. It's the single most important metric for understanding your risk tolerance and the health of your trading.

In my first year, my maximum drawdown was 60%. That meant my $5,000 account dropped to $2,000 at its lowest point. To recover, I needed a 150% gain — which took me months. Those were painful months.

In this guide, I'll explain exactly what maximum drawdown is, how to calculate it, and why keeping it small is the key to long-term survival.

💡 Key Takeaway: Your maximum drawdown determines how much pain you can endure and how quickly you can recover. Keep it below 20% at all costs.

What is Maximum Drawdown?

Maximum drawdown (MDD) is the largest peak-to-trough decline your account has ever experienced. It's the worst period in your trading history — the deepest hole you've ever dug yourself into.

Example: Over 12 months, your account had these peaks and troughs:

  • Peak: $12,000 → Trough: $10,000 → Drawdown: 16.7%
  • Peak: $11,500 → Trough: $8,500 → Drawdown: 26.1%
  • Peak: $10,000 → Trough: $7,000 → Drawdown: 30% ← This is your maximum drawdown
📊 Important: Maximum drawdown is measured from the highest peak, not from your starting balance. If you grow your account first, then lose, your MDD is calculated from that higher peak.

How to Calculate Maximum Drawdown

Formula: Maximum Drawdown = (Peak Balance − Lowest Trough) ÷ Peak Balance × 100%

Example Calculation:

Your account history:

  • Start: $10,000
  • Grow to: $15,000 (peak #1)
  • Drop to: $11,000 (trough) → Drawdown = ($15,000 - $11,000) ÷ $15,000 = 26.7%
  • Grow to: $18,000 (peak #2 — new high)
  • Drop to: $13,000 (trough) → Drawdown = ($18,000 - $13,000) ÷ $18,000 = 27.8%
  • Grow to: $20,000 (peak #3)
  • Drop to: $14,000 (trough) → Drawdown = ($20,000 - $14,000) ÷ $20,000 = 30%

Your maximum drawdown = 30% (the largest drop from any peak)

⚠️ Reality Check: Many traders don't track their maximum drawdown. That's like driving a car without a speedometer. You need this number to know if you're risking too much.

The Brutal Math of Recovery

Here's what every trader must understand: the deeper your drawdown, the harder it is to recover.

Drawdown % Gain Needed to Recover Difficulty
10% 11.1% Easy
20% 25% Moderate
30% 42.9% Difficult
40% 66.7% Very difficult
50% 100% Extremely difficult
75% 300% Near impossible
📌 Key Insight: A 50% drawdown requires a 100% gain to recover. That's twice as hard. A 75% drawdown requires a 300% gain — which takes years, if ever. This is why keeping drawdown small is non-negotiable.

What is an Acceptable Maximum Drawdown?

There's no universal answer, but here are my guidelines based on account size and experience:

Account Size Acceptable Max Drawdown Action Required
$100 — $1,000 15-20% Review risk management
$1,000 — $5,000 10-15% Reduce position sizes
$5,000 — $20,000 8-12% Stop trading, review everything
$20,000+ 5-8% Professional level — no excuses
⚠️ My Standard: I don't allow my account to go beyond 15% maximum drawdown. If I hit 10%, I reduce size by 50%. At 15%, I stop trading for a week to review my strategy.

How to Control Maximum Drawdown

1. Reduce Position Sizes

The most direct way to limit drawdown is to trade smaller. If you're in drawdown, reduce your position sizes by 50-75% until you recover.

2. Set Hard Stop Limits

Decide in advance: "If I hit 10% drawdown, I stop trading for 3 days." Write it down. Follow it.

3. Track Your Drawdown Daily

You can't control what you don't measure. Keep a spreadsheet or use your platform's reporting tools to track your MDD.

4. Review After Every Losing Streak

After 3 consecutive losses, stop and review. Are you following your plan? Is the market conditions changed?

5. Use Consistent Risk Per Trade

Risking 1% per trade keeps your drawdown predictable. Risking 5% makes deep drawdowns almost certain.

📌 Pro Tip: Create a "drawdown action plan" before you need it. Mine says: "At 5% drawdown, review all trades. At 10%, reduce size by 50%. At 15%, stop trading for one week."

My Drawdown Management Rules

Here are my strict rules for managing maximum drawdown:

  • 5% drawdown: Review all recent trades. No changes yet, but stay alert.
  • 10% drawdown: Reduce position sizes by 50%. Stop trading if emotional.
  • 15% drawdown: Stop trading entirely. Review every trade from the last 2 weeks. Identify the problem.
  • 20% drawdown: Return to demo account. Don't trade live again until profitable on demo for 1 month.
⚠️ The Bottom Line: Your maximum drawdown is a reflection of your risk management quality. Keep it below 20%. Track it daily. Have a plan for when it grows. Do this, and you'll survive long enough to become profitable.
