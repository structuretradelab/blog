Understanding Drawdown: What It Is and How to Control It

📅 Published: May 2026 📖 8 min read 🏷️ Category: Risk Management

Here's a number that most traders don't track — but should: drawdown. It's the measure of how much you're down from your account's highest point. And it's one of the most important indicators of your risk management health.

In this guide, I'll explain exactly what drawdown is, how to calculate it, and most importantly — how to control it.

💡 Key Takeaway: Drawdown is inevitable. Every trader experiences it. The key is keeping it small enough that you can recover.

What is Drawdown in Forex?

Drawdown is the peak-to-trough decline in your account balance. It measures how much money you've lost from your highest point (peak) to your lowest point (trough) before recovering.

Example:

  • Your account grows from $10,000 to $12,000 (peak = $12,000)
  • Then you lose money and it drops to $9,000 (trough = $9,000)
  • Drawdown = ($12,000 - $9,000) ÷ $12,000 = 25%
📊 Important: Drawdown is measured from the highest peak, not from your starting balance. If you grow your account first, then lose, your drawdown is calculated from that higher peak.

How to Calculate Drawdown

Formula: Drawdown = (Peak Balance − Trough Balance) ÷ Peak Balance × 100%

Examples:

Peak Balance Trough Balance Loss Amount Drawdown %
$10,000 $9,000 $1,000 10%
$10,000 $7,000 $3,000 30%
$15,000 $10,000 $5,000 33.3%
$8,000 $5,000 $3,000 37.5%

Maximum Drawdown Explained

Maximum drawdown is the largest peak-to-trough decline your account has ever experienced. It's the worst period in your trading history.

Example: Over 6 months, your account had these drawdowns:

  • February: 5% drawdown
  • April: 12% drawdown
  • June: 25% drawdown ← This is your maximum drawdown
⚠️ Why it matters: Your maximum drawdown tells you how much pain you're willing to endure. If it's 50%, you need a 100% gain just to get back to breakeven. That's why limiting drawdown is critical.

Why Drawdown Matters

  • It shows your true risk: Your risk per trade (1%) is theoretical. Your drawdown is real.
  • It affects your psychology: Deep drawdowns lead to fear, hesitation, and bad decisions.
  • It determines recovery time: A 10% drawdown needs 11% gain to recover. A 50% drawdown needs 100% gain. Bigger drawdowns are exponentially harder to recover from.
  • It's a reality check: If your max drawdown is 40%, you're risking too much. Period.
📌 Recovery Math:
• 10% drawdown → need 11% gain to recover
• 20% drawdown → need 25% gain to recover
• 30% drawdown → need 43% gain to recover
• 50% drawdown → need 100% gain to recover
• 80% drawdown → need 400% gain to recover

What is an Acceptable Drawdown?

There's no single answer, but here are general guidelines:

Drawdown Level Risk Level Recommendation
1-10% Low risk Excellent. Professional level.
11-20% Moderate risk Good. Keep monitoring.
21-30% High risk Review your risk management. Reduce position sizes.
31-50% Very high risk Stop trading. Re-evaluate your entire strategy.
⚠️ My Rule: I stop trading for the week if I hit a 10% drawdown. I stop trading for the month if I hit 20%. This rule has saved my account multiple times.

How to Control and Limit Drawdown

1. Reduce Position Sizes

If you're in drawdown, your first instinct might be to trade larger to recover faster. That's the worst thing you can do. Reduce your position sizes by 50% until you recover.

2. Set Daily and Weekly Loss Limits

Stop trading for the day after losing 2-3%. Stop for the week after 5-6%. This prevents one bad period from becoming catastrophic.

3. Use Consistent Risk Per Trade

Risk 1% per trade. No exceptions. This keeps drawdowns small and predictable.

4. Track Your Drawdown

Write down your drawdown after every losing streak. You can't control what you don't measure.

5. Take a Break

After a significant drawdown, step away from the screen for a few days. Clear your head. Come back fresh.

📌 Pro Tip: Create a "drawdown action plan" before you need it. Mine says: "At 10% drawdown, stop trading for 3 days. Reduce position sizes by 50% for the next 10 trades. Review every trade in my journal before re-entering."

Drawdown is Part of Trading

Every trader experiences drawdown. Even the best traders in the world have losing streaks. The difference is they keep their drawdowns small and recoverable.

I've learned to accept drawdown as part of the process. I don't fight it. I don't trade larger to recover. I follow my plan, reduce my size, and let the probability work.

Keep your drawdown below 20%. Track it daily. Have a plan for when it happens. Do this, and you'll survive long enough to become profitable.
