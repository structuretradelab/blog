Stop Loss Explained: Why Every Trade Needs One

📅 Published: May 2026 📖 8 min read 🏷️ Category: Risk Management

If you take only one lesson from my trading, let it be this: always use a stop loss. Every trade. No exceptions.

I learned this the hard way. In my first month of trading, I had a trade go 50 pips against me. I didn't have a stop loss. "It will come back," I told myself. It didn't. It went 200 pips against me. I lost 40% of my account on one trade.

A stop loss would have saved me. Now, I never enter a trade without one.

What is a Stop Loss?

A stop loss is an order to automatically close a trade at a predetermined price if the market moves against you. It's your exit plan for losing trades.

Example: You buy EUR/USD at 1.1050. You set a stop loss at 1.1030. If the price drops to 1.1030, your trade closes automatically. Your loss is limited to 20 pips.

📊 Without a Stop Loss: You buy at 1.1050. Price drops to 1.1000. You lose 50 pips. Price drops to 1.0950. You lose 100 pips. Without a stop loss, you have no limit on your loss.

Why Stop Losses Are Essential

  • They limit your losses: You decide exactly how much you're willing to lose before entering the trade.
  • They remove emotion: When price hits your stop, the trade closes automatically. No "maybe it will come back" decisions.
  • They protect against margin calls: A stop loss prevents catastrophic losses that trigger margin calls.
  • They let you sleep at night: You know your maximum loss. You don't have to watch every tick.
  • They make you a trader, not a gambler: Gamblers hope. Traders plan their exit.
⚠️ The Hard Truth: Every trader who has blown an account did so because they ignored stop losses. Don't be one of them.

How to Set a Stop Loss

There are two main ways to set a stop loss:

1. Fixed Pip Stop Loss

Set a fixed number of pips below your entry. Example: Buy EUR/USD at 1.1050, stop loss 20 pips below at 1.1030.

Pros: Simple, easy to calculate position size.
Cons: Doesn't account for market structure.

2. Technical Stop Loss

Place your stop beyond a key support/resistance level or recent swing low/high.

Pros: Respects market structure. Less likely to be hit by random noise.
Cons: Requires more analysis.

📌 Recommendation: As a beginner, start with fixed pip stop losses (20-30 pips on EUR/USD). As you gain experience, transition to technical stop losses.

Stop Loss Placement Strategies

StrategyPlacementBest For
Below Swing Low (Long) Just below the most recent support level Trend followers, breakout traders
Above Swing High (Short) Just above the most recent resistance level Trend followers, breakout traders
Below Moving Average Below a key moving average (e.g., 50 EMA) MA crossover traders
Fixed Pip Stop 15-30 pips for EUR/USD, 30-50 for GBP/JPY Beginners, scalpers
⚠️ Avoid putting stops at obvious round numbers: 1.1000, 1.1050, 1.1100. Big traders hunt these levels. Place your stop 2-3 pips beyond instead (e.g., 1.0997 instead of 1.1000).

Common Stop Loss Mistakes

  • No stop loss at all: The #1 mistake. Trading without a stop loss is gambling.
  • Moving your stop loss further away: "I'll give it a little more room." This turns a small loss into a big loss. Never move your stop further away.
  • Stop loss too tight: Set so close that normal volatility triggers it. Give the trade room to breathe.
  • Stop loss too wide: Risking too much on one trade. Use your 1% rule to determine max stop distance.
  • Emotional stop placement: "I feel like it will stop here." Use technical levels or fixed pip distances, not feelings.
📌 The Golden Rule: Never, ever move your stop loss further away from entry. You can move it closer to lock in profits (trailing stop). But moving it away is how accounts get blown.

What About Trailing Stops?

A trailing stop automatically moves your stop loss in the direction of the trade as price moves in your favor. It locks in profits while still protecting against reversals.

Example: You buy EUR/USD at 1.1000 with a 20 pip trailing stop. Price rises to 1.1020. Your stop moves to 1.1000 (breakeven). Price rises to 1.1050. Your stop moves to 1.1030 (20 pips profit locked in).

📌 Recommendation: Trailing stops are great for trending markets. But as a beginner, focus on basic stop losses first. Add trailing stops after 6-12 months of experience.

The Stop Loss is Your Best Friend

Every trader who has survived more than a year uses stop losses. Every trader who has blown an account has ignored them at some point.

After 6 years, I place a stop loss before I enter every single trade. It takes 5 seconds. It saves me from catastrophic losses. It lets me sleep at night.

Set a stop loss before every trade. Never move it further away. Keep your risk at 1-2%. Do this, and you'll survive long enough to become profitable.
