Risk Reward Ratio: How to Calculate and Why 1:2 is Minimum

📅 Published: May 2026 📖 8 min read 🏷️ Category: Risk Management

Here's a question that changed my trading forever: "If you risk 1% per trade, how many trades can you lose in a row before you're out of the game?"

The answer depends on your risk reward ratio — the relationship between how much you risk and how much you stand to gain. Most traders ignore this. Profitable traders live by it.

In this guide, I'll explain exactly what risk reward ratio is, how to calculate it, and why 1:2 should be your absolute minimum for every trade.

💡 Key Takeaway: With a 1:2 risk reward ratio, you only need to win 34% of your trades to be profitable. With 1:1, you need 51%. The math favors patience and discipline.

What is Risk Reward Ratio?

Risk reward ratio compares how much you stand to lose (risk) to how much you stand to gain (reward) on a single trade.

Example: You risk $100 to make $200. Your risk reward ratio is 1:2.

Formula: Risk Reward Ratio = (Potential Loss) : (Potential Profit)

📊 Put simply: How many dollars are you risking to make one dollar? 1:2 means you risk $1 to make $2. 1:1 means you risk $1 to make $1.

How to Calculate Risk Reward

Step 1: Determine your risk in pips
Stop loss distance = 20 pips

Step 2: Determine your target in pips
Take profit distance = 40 pips

Step 3: Calculate the ratio
40 ÷ 20 = 2 → 1:2 risk reward

Why Risk Reward Matters More Than Win Rate

Most beginners obsess over win rate. "I won 70% of my trades!" But a high win rate doesn't matter if your losses are bigger than your wins.

Example:

  • Trader A: 70% win rate, but wins 10 pips and loses 50 pips. Net result after 10 trades: 7 wins (70 pips) − 3 losses (150 pips) = -80 pips
  • Trader B: 40% win rate, but wins 50 pips and loses 20 pips. Net result after 10 trades: 4 wins (200 pips) − 6 losses (120 pips) = +80 pips
⚠️ The Hard Truth: Win rate without risk reward is meaningless. You can be profitable with a 40% win rate — if your winners are bigger than your losers.

Why 1:2 is the Minimum

After 6 years, I never take a trade with less than 1:2 risk reward. Here's why:

  • It gives you room for error: You can lose 2 out of 3 trades and still break even.
  • It reduces emotional pressure: You don't need to be right most of the time. One winner can cover two losers.
  • It forces patience: You wait for quality setups with clear targets, not random trades.
  • It aligns with probability: Even a coin flip gives you 50% win rate. You don't need to be a genius.
⚠️ My Rule: If a trade doesn't offer at least 1:2 risk reward, I don't take it. Period. No exceptions. It's better to sit on my hands than to take a bad trade.

How to Improve Your Risk Reward

1. Widen Your Take Profit

Set your profit target at logical resistance levels, not arbitrary pips. Let winners run.

2. Tighten Your Stop Loss

Place stops beyond key support/resistance, not random distances. A tighter stop improves your ratio.

3. Be More Selective

Don't trade every setup. Wait for high-probability setups with clear 1:2 or 1:3 potential.

4. Use Multiple Time Frames

A trade that looks like 1:1 on a 5-minute chart might be 1:3 on a 1-hour chart. Zoom out.

📌 Pro Tip: Before entering any trade, write down your risk (stop loss) and reward (take profit). If the ratio isn't at least 1:2, walk away. This one habit changed my trading more than any strategy.

Let the Math Work for You

Trading is a game of probability. You don't need to be right most of the time. You just need your winners to be bigger than your losers.

I don't worry about my win rate. Some months I win 40% of my trades. Some months 55%. But because my average winner is 2-3 times larger than my average loser, I'm consistently profitable.

Set your stop loss. Set your take profit. Calculate your risk reward. Never take a trade below 1:2. Do this, and the math will work in your favor.
