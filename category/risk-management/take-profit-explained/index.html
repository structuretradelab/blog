Take Profit Explained: How to Set Profit Targets

📅 Published: May 2026 📖 7 min read 🏷️ Category: Risk Management

You've entered a trade. You've set your stop loss. Now comes the next question: where do you take profit?

Early in my trading, I had no answer to this question. I'd let trades run until they turned against me. I'd take profits too early and watch price go further without me. I had no plan.

A take profit order is your exit plan for winning trades. It automatically closes your trade when price reaches your profit target. Without it, you're leaving your profits to chance.

💡 Key Takeaway: Always set a take profit before entering a trade. "The trend is your friend" is dangerous advice without a profit target.

What is a Take Profit Order?

A take profit (TP) is an order to automatically close a trade at a predetermined price when the trade becomes profitable.

Example: You buy EUR/USD at 1.1050. You set a take profit at 1.1080. If price rises to 1.1080, your trade closes automatically. Your profit is 30 pips.

📊 Without a Take Profit: You buy at 1.1050. Price rises to 1.1080 (+30 pips), then falls back to 1.1050. You made nothing. A take profit locks in your gain.

Why Take Profits Are Essential

  • They lock in profits: Markets reverse. A take profit ensures you exit while you're ahead.
  • They remove emotion: No "should I hold longer?" decisions. The plan executes automatically.
  • They enforce discipline: You decide your profit target before entering, not during the trade.
  • They complete your risk-reward ratio: A stop loss without a take profit is incomplete.
  • They let you walk away: Set your TP and SL, then step away from the screen.
⚠️ The Hard Truth: Greed is why most traders give back profits. A take profit order protects you from yourself.

The Risk Reward Connection

Your take profit determines your risk-reward ratio — how much you stand to gain versus how much you risk.

Formula: Risk Reward = (Take Profit Distance) ÷ (Stop Loss Distance)

Example: Stop loss at 20 pips, take profit at 40 pips → Risk Reward = 40 ÷ 20 = 2 (1:2 ratio).

Risk Reward Win Rate Needed to Break Even Best For
1:1 50% Scalpers, very high win rate strategies
1:2 33.3% Recommended minimum for most traders
1:3 25% Swing traders, trend followers
📌 My Standard: I never take a trade with less than 1:2 risk reward. Most of my trades target 1:3. This means I can be wrong more than half the time and still be profitable.

How to Set Take Profit Levels

1. Fixed Pip Take Profit

Set a fixed number of pips above your entry. Example: Buy at 1.1050, TP 30 pips at 1.1080.

Pros: Simple, easy to calculate.
Cons: Doesn't account for market structure.

2. Technical Take Profit

Place your TP at key resistance levels (for long trades) or support levels (for short trades).

Pros: Respects market structure. Price often reverses at these levels.
Cons: Requires more analysis.

3. Risk Multiplier

Set TP based on your stop loss distance. Example: SL = 20 pips, set TP = 40-60 pips (2x-3x risk).

Pros: Maintains consistent risk reward.
Cons: May not align with market structure.

📌 Recommendation: As a beginner, start with risk multiplier (2x or 3x your stop loss). This keeps your risk reward consistent while you learn.

Common Take Profit Mistakes

  • No take profit at all: Leaving profits to chance. Markets reverse without warning.
  • Moving take profit further away (greed): "It's going higher!" Then price reverses and you lose the profit you had.
  • Take profit too close: Risking 20 pips to make 10 pips. Negative risk reward.
  • Moving take profit closer (fear): Closing too early, leaving money on the table.
  • No risk reward calculation: Taking trades without knowing your potential return.
⚠️ The Golden Rule: Never move your take profit further away out of greed. You can move it closer to lock in profits. But moving it away is how profits turn into losses.

Should You Take Partial Profits?

Partial profit taking means closing part of your position at one level and letting the rest run.

Example: You buy 2 lots. You set TP1 at 20 pips (closes 1 lot). You set TP2 at 40 pips (closes 1 lot).

Pros: Locks in some profit while still allowing for larger moves.
Cons: More complex, requires managing multiple orders.

📌 Recommendation: As a beginner, use a single take profit. It's simpler and removes decision fatigue. Add partial profit taking after 6-12 months of experience.

Know Your Exit Before You Enter

Every trade needs three things: entry, stop loss, and take profit. Without all three, you don't have a trade — you have a gamble.

I set my take profit before I enter every single trade. It's part of my trading plan. I know exactly how much I stand to gain before I risk a single dollar.

Set your take profit before every trade. Maintain a minimum 1:2 risk reward. Never move your take profit further away out of greed. Do this, and you'll turn probability in your favor.
