What is a Drawdown?

A drawdown is the peak-to-trough decline in your account balance. It measures how much money you've lost from your highest point (peak) to your lowest point (trough) before recovering .

Example: Your account grows from $10,000 to $12,000 (peak = $12,000). Then you lose money and it drops to $9,000 (trough = $9,000). Your drawdown is 25% .

Drawdown % = (Peak - Trough) ÷ Peak × 100

📌 Key Insight: A 20% drawdown means you've lost 20% of your account from its highest point. To recover, you need a 25% gain from the bottom, not 20%.

The Math of Recovery

Here's the math that most traders don't understand:

Drawdown Gain Needed to Recover
5% 5.3%
10% 11.1%
20% 25%
30% 42.9%
40% 66.7%
50% 100%

Why 20% needs 25%: If your account drops from $10,000 to $8,000 (20% loss), you need to gain $2,000 to return to $10,000. That's a 25% gain from $8,000 .

⚠️ The Hard Truth: The deeper your drawdown, the harder it is to recover. A 20% drawdown needs 25% gain. A 50% drawdown needs 100% gain. This is why preventing deep drawdowns is critical.

Why Rushing Makes It Worse

When traders hit a drawdown, they often make the same mistake: they try to recover quickly by increasing risk .

The problem:

  • Emotional decision-making: Fear and frustration drive you to take unnecessary risks
  • Increased position sizes: You risk more to "win it back" — but one more loss makes it worse
  • Lower-quality setups: You start taking trades that don't match your strategy
  • Revenge trading: You feel the need to "get even" with the market

Result: You turn a 20% drawdown into a 30% or 40% drawdown .

📌 The Golden Rule: Never increase your risk to recover from a drawdown. The goal is to survive, not to get rich quickly.

Step-by-Step Recovery Plan

Here's the exact plan to follow when you hit a 20% drawdown:

Step 1: Stop Trading

Action: Close all open positions. Stop trading for at least 24-48 hours .

Why: You need to reset mentally. Trading while emotional leads to more losses .

Step 2: Review Your Trades

Action: Review every trade that contributed to the drawdown. Ask yourself:

  • Did I follow my trading plan? If yes, the drawdown is part of the process. If no, identify what broke.
  • Was I overtrading? Did I take too many trades, too quickly, or of lower quality?
  • Was I risking more than 1% per trade? Even one or two oversized trades can cause a major drawdown .

Step 3: Reduce Position Size

Action: Reduce your position size by 50% .

Why: Smaller positions reduce the emotional impact of losses and let you recover safely .

Step 4: Reduce Your Risk Per Trade

Action: Reduce your risk from 1% to 0.5% per trade.

Why: A series of small wins at 0.5% risk will recover your account gradually .

Step 5: Focus on Quality, Not Quantity

Action: Only take A+ setups that meet all your criteria. Skip any trade that's not perfect.

Why: You need to rebuild confidence. Winning a few high-quality trades is better than many low-quality ones.

Step 6: Journal Every Trade

Action: Log every trade in your journal. Include your emotional state and whether you followed your plan.

Why: Journaling helps you maintain discipline and learn from your recovery process .

Step 7: Set a Realistic Timeline

Action: Plan to recover over 1-3 months, not 1-3 days .

📌 Recovery Timeline: If you risk 0.5% per trade and gain 5-10% per month, a 20% drawdown could take 2-4 months to recover. This is normal. Patience is key.

Adjusting Your Strategy for Recovery

After a 20% drawdown, it's worth reviewing your strategy:

Option 1: Reduce Frequency

If you're overtrading, reduce the number of trades you take. Try focusing on one trade per day or only the highest-quality setups .

Option 2: Adjust Your Risk Reward

If your win rate is high but you're still losing, your risk reward ratio may be too low. Aim for 1:2 minimum .

Option 3: Widen Your Stop Loss

If you're getting stopped out by noise, consider widening your stop loss or using ATR-based stops .

Option 4: Test Before Returning

Before returning to live trading, test your adjusted strategy on a demo account for at least 2 weeks .

⚠️ Important: Don't make major changes to your strategy during a drawdown. Emotional decisions lead to bad changes. Wait until you're calm, then review objectively.

The Right Mindset

Recovering from a drawdown is as much mental as it is technical. Here's the mindset you need:

1. Accept That Drawdowns Are Part of Trading

Every trader experiences drawdowns. Even the best traders have losing streaks . Accepting this removes the emotional pressure to "get even" with the market.

2. Trust Your Process, Not Your Emotions

If you followed your plan, the drawdown is just statistical variance. Trust your process and continue executing.

3. Focus on the Process, Not the Profits

Don't focus on recovering your losses. Focus on executing your plan correctly. The profits will follow .

4. Be Patient

Recovery takes time. Rushing leads to more mistakes. Be patient. Small consistent gains add up .

📌 The Professional's Mindset: A drawdown is not failure. It's tuition. You learn more from losses than from wins. Use this drawdown as a learning opportunity, not a punishment.

How to Prevent Future Drawdowns

Here's how to prevent deep drawdowns in the future:

  • Always risk 1% per trade: This is the single most important rule. It caps your downside.
  • Set a daily loss limit: Stop trading after losing 2-3% in a single day.
  • Set a weekly loss limit: Stop trading after losing 5-6% in a week.
  • Reduce position size after losses: If you're down 5%, reduce size by 50%.
  • Review your journal regularly: Identify patterns that lead to drawdowns and correct them.
⚠️ My Rule: I stop trading for the week if I hit a 5% drawdown. I review all my trades and only return after I've identified the problem. This rule has saved my account multiple times.

Drawdowns Are Temporary. Discipline Is Forever.

A 20% drawdown feels devastating. But it's not the end. It's a signal to review, adjust, and improve. The traders who succeed are the ones who learn from their drawdowns and come back stronger.

Stop trading. Review your mistakes. Reduce your position size. Reduce your risk to 0.5%. Focus on quality trades. Be patient. Recover slowly. That's how you bounce back stronger than before.
