Every trade has three decisions: where to enter, where to place your stop loss, and where to take profit.

Most beginners focus on the entry. They spend hours analyzing charts, looking for the perfect setup. But they spend almost no time thinking about their exit. They place their stop loss at a random level and their take profit at a "round number." Then they wonder why they keep losing.

In this guide, let's walk through how to use a stop loss and take profit together — so your exits are as strategic as your entries.

💡 Key Takeaway: Your stop loss and take profit should be placed based on market structure, not random numbers. Together, they define your risk reward ratio — the single most important metric for long-term profitability.

What Are Stop Loss and Take Profit?

Stop Loss (SL): An order that automatically closes your trade if price moves against you. It limits your loss to a predetermined amount.

Take Profit (TP): An order that automatically closes your trade when price reaches your profit target. It locks in your profit without you having to watch the screen.

Together, they define:

  • Your maximum loss on the trade (SL)
  • Your maximum gain on the trade (TP)
  • Your risk reward ratio (TP distance ÷ SL distance)
📊 Simple Analogy: Think of it like a car trip. The stop loss is the emergency brake — it keeps you from going off a cliff. The take profit is your destination — it tells you when to stop driving and get out.

Why They Work Together

Stop loss and take profit are not independent decisions. They are two sides of the same coin.

  • Without SL, one bad trade can wipe you out. No matter how good your analysis, the market can move against you. The SL protects your capital.
  • Without TP, you give back your profits. Markets reverse. Without a target, you'll hold winning trades until they turn into losing trades.
  • Together, they create a positive expectancy. When your TP is at least 2x your SL, you can be profitable even with a moderate win rate.
⚠️ The Rule: Never enter a trade without BOTH a stop loss AND a take profit. No exceptions. If you can't define both, don't take the trade.

How to Set Your Stop Loss

Your stop loss should be placed where your trading thesis is proven wrong.

For a Long Trade (Buy)

  • Place your stop loss just below a recent swing low or support level
  • Give it 5-10 pips of room beyond the level to avoid noise
  • Never place it at an obvious round number

For a Short Trade (Sell)

  • Place your stop loss just above a recent swing high or resistance level
  • Give it 5-10 pips of room beyond the level to avoid noise
  • Avoid obvious round numbers

Example: If you buy EUR/USD at 1.1050 and recent support is at 1.1030, place your stop loss at 1.1025 (5 pips below support). Your stop distance is 25 pips.

📌 Pro Tip: Use ATR (Average True Range) to adjust your stop loss based on volatility. If ATR is high, widen your stop. If ATR is low, tighten it. This prevents getting stopped out by normal noise.

How to Set Your Take Profit

Your take profit should be placed at the next logical level where price is likely to reverse or stall.

Common TP Levels

  • Support/resistance levels: The next level where price may reverse
  • Swing highs/lows: Previous peaks and troughs
  • Fibonacci levels: 1.272, 1.618 extensions
  • Round numbers: 1.1100, 1.1200, etc.
  • Measured move: The distance from pattern height (for chart patterns)

Example: If you buy EUR/USD at 1.1050 with support at 1.1030 (SL at 1.1025, 25 pips), your TP should be at least 50 pips away (1:2 ratio). You look for the next resistance level — say 1.1100 (50 pips away). TP is set at 1.1100.

⚠️ Important: Your TP must give you at least a 1:2 risk reward ratio. If the next logical TP level is only 30 pips away and your SL is 25 pips, that's only 1:1.2 — not good enough. Skip the trade.

The Relationship: Risk Reward Ratio

Your stop loss and take profit define your risk reward ratio (RRR).

Risk Reward Ratio = TP Distance ÷ SL Distance

Examples:

  • SL: 25 pips, TP: 50 pips → RRR = 1:2 ✅
  • SL: 25 pips, TP: 75 pips → RRR = 1:3 ✅
  • SL: 25 pips, TP: 25 pips → RRR = 1:1 ❌ (not recommended)
  • SL: 25 pips, TP: 15 pips → RRR = 1:0.6 ❌ (terrible)

The math: With 1:2 RRR, you only need a 34% win rate to break even. With 1:3 RRR, you only need 26%. With 1:1, you need 51%.

📌 My Rule: I never take a trade with less than 1:2 risk reward. Most of my trades target 1:3. This means I can be wrong more than half the time and still be profitable.

Real Examples

Example 1: EUR/USD Long Trade

  • Entry: 1.1050
  • Stop Loss: 1.1025 (25 pips below entry)
  • Take Profit: 1.1100 (50 pips above entry, next resistance)
  • Risk Reward Ratio: 1:2
  • Result: If TP hits, you make 2x what you would lose if SL hits

Example 2: GBP/USD Short Trade

  • Entry: 1.2800
  • Stop Loss: 1.2830 (30 pips above entry)
  • Take Profit: 1.2710 (90 pips below entry, swing low)
  • Risk Reward Ratio: 1:3
  • Result: You only need to win 26% of these trades to break even

Example 3: USD/JPY Long Trade

  • Entry: 150.00
  • Stop Loss: 149.70 (30 pips below entry)
  • Take Profit: 150.60 (60 pips above entry)
  • Risk Reward Ratio: 1:2
  • Result: If TP hits, you make 2x your risk

Common Mistakes

1. Moving your stop loss further away

This is the #1 account killer. You set a stop, price approaches it, and you move it "just a little more." Then the price blows through and you lose much more than planned. Never move your stop further away.

2. Moving your take profit further away

Greed makes you move your TP to "let it run." Price reverses before hitting the new target, and you end up with nothing. Don't move your TP. Take what you planned.

3. Setting TP too close

If your TP is only 10 pips away and your SL is 30 pips, your RRR is 1:0.33. You need an 75% win rate just to break even. Not good.

4. Ignoring market structure

Placing SL and TP at random pip distances. The market doesn't care about your "20-pip stop." It respects structure — support, resistance, swing points. Place your exits accordingly.

5. Using no stop loss at all

Some traders think they can watch the trade and close it manually. But emotions take over. You'll hold losers hoping for a reversal. Always use an automated stop loss.

⚠️ The Bottom Line: Your stop loss and take profit are not optional. They are the foundation of every trade. Place them before you enter. Never move them. Let the market do its thing.

Plan Your Exit Before You Enter

Every trade should have three things: entry, stop loss, and take profit. Without all three, you don't have a trade — you have a gamble.

Set both SL and TP before you enter. Place them on market structure. Maintain 1:2 minimum. Never move them. That's how you turn probability in your favor.
