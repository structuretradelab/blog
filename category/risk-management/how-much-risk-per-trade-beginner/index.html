The 1% Rule: Your Survival Guarantee

The 1% rule is simple: never risk more than 1% of your account balance on any single trade.

How it works:

  • If you have a $10,000 account, your maximum loss per trade is $100
  • If you have a $1,000 account, your maximum loss per trade is $10
  • If you have a $100 account, your maximum loss per trade is $1

Why 1%? Because it allows you to survive long losing streaks. Even if you lose 10 trades in a row, you still have 90% of your account left.

📌 The Golden Rule: Risk 1% per trade. Always. No exceptions. This is the single most important rule in trading.

Why 1% is the Golden Standard

Most successful traders risk 1-2% per trade. Here's why:

  • Survival: You can survive a losing streak without blowing your account
  • Emotional control: Small losses don't trigger fear or revenge trading
  • Consistency: The same risk every trade builds discipline
  • Compounding: Small consistent gains add up over time
  • Recovery: A 10% drawdown requires 11% gain to recover — manageable with 1% risk
⚠️ The Hard Truth: Most beginners blow their accounts because they risk too much. The 1% rule is the difference between surviving and blowing up.

How to Calculate Your Risk Per Trade

Here's the formula for calculating your risk per trade:

Risk Amount = Account Balance × Risk Percentage

Example:

  • Account Balance: $5,000
  • Risk Percentage: 1%
  • Risk Amount: $5,000 × 0.01 = $50

To find your position size:

Position Size = Risk Amount ÷ (Stop Loss in Pips × Pip Value)

Complete example:

  • Account Balance: $5,000
  • Risk: 1% ($50)
  • Stop Loss: 20 pips
  • Pip Value (micro lot): $0.10
  • Position Size = $50 ÷ (20 × $0.10) = $50 ÷ $2 = 0.25 lots

When (and When Not) to Adjust Your Risk

When to Reduce Risk

  • During drawdown: If you're down 10%, reduce risk to 0.5% until you recover
  • When you're emotional: Fear, greed, or revenge trading means risk should be lower
  • During volatile markets: News events and high volatility require smaller positions
  • When you're learning: Beginners should risk 1%, not 2%

When NOT to Increase Risk

  • After wins: Greed makes you want to risk more — don't
  • After losses: Revenge trading means risk more — don't
  • When you're on a streak: Past performance doesn't guarantee future results
  • When you're stressed: Emotional trading leads to mistakes
