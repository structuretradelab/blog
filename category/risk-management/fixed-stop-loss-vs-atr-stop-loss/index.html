You've learned how to place a stop loss. But now you're facing a new question: Should I use a fixed stop loss or an ATR-based stop loss?

This is a common dilemma for traders. Both methods have their strengths and weaknesses. The right choice depends on your trading style, the markets you trade, and your personal preferences.

In this guide, let's walk through the differences between fixed and ATR stops, when to use each, and how to combine them for better results.

💡 Key Takeaway: The best stop-loss method aligns with how you enter trades. If you're trading volatility-based strategies, use ATR. If you're trading structure-based strategies, use technical stops. The key is consistency — stick to one approach or combine them deliberately.

What is a Fixed Stop Loss?

A fixed stop loss is a predetermined number of pips or points placed at a set distance from your entry, regardless of what the market is doing at the time. If you always risk 30 pips on a EUR/USD trade, that's a fixed stop.

The appeal is obvious: simplicity. You know your worst-case loss before you enter the trade. Position sizing is straightforward. Backtesting is clean.

The problem: Markets move differently depending on the session, news environment, and instrument. A 30-pip stop that works during a quiet London morning will get clipped constantly during a volatile New York session.

📌 Example: You set a 20-pip fixed stop on EUR/USD. During the Asian session, that might be plenty of room. But during NFP news, a 20-pip stop will be hit within seconds — even if your analysis was correct.

What is an ATR Stop Loss?

An ATR stop loss uses the Average True Range (ATR) indicator to measure market volatility and adjust your stop distance accordingly.

How it works: When you multiply ATR by a specific factor, you create a volatility-adjusted stop that scales with the current market conditions.

Formula:

Long Stop = Entry − (ATR × Multiplier)

Short Stop = Entry + (ATR × Multiplier)

Example: If ATR(14) on EUR/USD is 30 pips and you use a 1.5x multiplier, your stop is placed 45 pips from entry. If volatility increases and ATR rises to 50 pips, your stop automatically widens to 75 pips.

📊 Key Advantage: ATR stops adapt to market conditions. In volatile conditions, your stop widens automatically. In calm conditions, it tightens. You're no longer fighting the market's natural rhythm — you're working with it.

When to Use a Fixed Stop

Fixed stops work best in specific situations:

  • Scalping strategies — with very short hold times, there's rarely time for complex stop calculations
  • Consistent market conditions — if volatility is relatively stable, fixed stops can work well
  • Single-instrument trading — if you only trade EUR/USD, you can dial in the right fixed distance for that pair
  • Beginners learning the mechanics — fixed stops are the simplest to implement
⚠️ Important: Fixed stops can be dangerous in volatile conditions. A 20-pip stop that works on a quiet day will get destroyed during news events. Always check market conditions before using a fixed stop.

When to Use an ATR Stop

ATR stops shine in these situations:

  • Multi-instrument trading — the same ATR formula works across different pairs, assets, and timeframes
  • Changing volatility conditions — when volatility varies significantly (news events, session changes)
  • Systematic strategies — traders who want a hands-off, rule-based sizing approach
  • Volatility-based entry strategies — if your entry method is tied to volatility, ATR stops align naturally

Common ATR multipliers:

Trading Style ATR Multiplier Why
Scalping 0.5x to 1.0x ATR Very tight, short hold times
Day Trading 1.5x to 2.0x ATR Balanced breathing room
Swing Trading 2.0x to 3.0x ATR Wider for larger moves
📌 Pro Tip: The ATR multiplier is not a fixed rule. Test different multipliers on your strategy to see which one works best. Start with 1.5x for day trading and 2.5x for swing trading.

The Best of Both Worlds: A Hybrid Approach

Many professional traders don't choose one or the other — they combine both methods.

Option 1: Structure + ATR Floor

Use structure-based stops (beyond support/resistance or swing lows/highs), but never tighter than 1× ATR. This prevents getting stopped out by noise inside the structure.

Option 2: ATR + Visual Sanity Check

Compute the ATR stop, then visually inspect it. If it sits in the middle of a recent range, adjust it to the nearest structural level.

Option 3: Enter with Structure, Trail with ATR

Enter with a structure-based stop. Once the trade is in profit, trail with an ATR-multiple behind price to capture trend moves.

📌 My Recommendation: Use structure-based stops (beyond support/resistance) with a minimum 1× ATR floor. This gives you the best of both worlds — respect for market structure and adaptation to volatility.

Choose What Fits Your Strategy

There's no universal "best" stop loss method. Fixed stops are simple and clean. ATR stops adapt to volatility. Structure stops respect market logic. The best approach depends on your trading style and strategy.

If you're new, start with fixed stops to learn the mechanics. Then experiment with ATR stops as you gain experience. Combine them when you're ready. That's how you find the right fit for your trading.
