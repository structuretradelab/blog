Every time you enter a trade, you start at a small disadvantage. That disadvantage is called the spread. It's how brokers make money — and it's your primary trading cost.

When I first started, I didn't understand spreads. I wondered why I was losing money immediately after opening a trade. Then I learned: the spread is the difference between the buy price (ask) and the sell price (bid).

In this guide, I'll explain what spreads are, the difference between fixed and variable spreads, and which one is better for beginners.

💡 Key Takeaway: Spreads are your trading cost. Lower spreads = lower costs. Major pairs like EUR/USD have the tightest spreads (often 0.0-0.5 pips).

What is Spread in Forex?

The spread is the difference between the ask price (what you pay to buy) and the bid price (what you receive when you sell).

Example: EUR/USD shows:

  • Bid price (sell): 1.1050
  • Ask price (buy): 1.1052
  • Spread = 2 pips

If you buy at 1.1052, you immediately lose 2 pips because you'd only sell at 1.1050. The spread is the broker's fee for executing your trade.

📊 Real Cost Example: If you trade 1 standard lot ($10 per pip) on EUR/USD with a 1 pip spread, you pay $10 in spread costs for that trade — win or lose.

Fixed Spreads: Predictable but Wider

Fixed spreads stay the same regardless of market conditions. The broker promises a consistent spread, usually 1-3 pips on major pairs.

Pros of fixed spreads:

  • Predictable costs — you know your spread before every trade
  • No surprises during news events
  • Easier for beginners to calculate costs

Cons of fixed spreads:

  • Usually wider than variable spreads during normal conditions
  • Brokers may refuse orders during high volatility (requotes)
  • Less common with top-tier brokers
⚠️ Note: Fixed spread brokers often have "dealing desk" execution, meaning they may reject your trades during fast-moving markets.

Variable Spreads: Tight but Fluctuating

Variable spreads (also called floating spreads) change based on market liquidity and volatility. During calm times, spreads can be extremely tight. During news events, spreads can widen significantly.

Pros of variable spreads:

  • Tightest spreads during normal conditions (0.0-0.5 pips on EUR/USD)
  • No dealing desk — direct market access
  • No requotes (trades execute instantly)
  • Preferred by scalpers and active traders

Cons of variable spreads:

  • Can widen dramatically during news events (5-20+ pips)
  • Less predictable costs
📊 Typical Variable Spreads (EUR/USD):
• London/New York overlap: 0.0 — 0.3 pips
• Asian session: 0.5 — 1.0 pips
• During major news (NFP, FOMC): 5 — 20+ pips

📌 My Recommendation: Choose variable spreads from a reputable broker. The tighter spreads save you money, and no dealing desk execution is more reliable. Just avoid trading during major news events when spreads widen.

When Do Spreads Widen?

Spread widening happens when liquidity decreases or volatility increases. Avoid trading during these times:

  • Major news releases: NFP (Non-Farm Payroll), FOMC rate decisions, CPI data, ECB press conferences
  • Session closes: Last hour of London session (11 AM — 12 PM EST), last hour of New York session (4 PM — 5 PM EST)
  • Weekend open: Sunday 5 PM EST — markets are thin
  • Bank holidays: When major financial centers are closed
⚠️ Pro Tip: Check an economic calendar before trading. If a major news event is coming in 10 minutes, stop trading. Wait until 15-20 minutes after the release when spreads normalize.

How to Minimize Spread Costs

Here's how to keep your trading costs low:

  • Trade major pairs only (EUR/USD, GBP/USD, USD/JPY) — They have the tightest spreads
  • Trade during London/New York overlap (8 AM — 12 PM EST) — Highest liquidity, tightest spreads
  • Avoid trading 10 minutes before and after major news — Spreads widen dramatically
  • Consider a Raw spread account — Ultra-tight spreads with small commission (often cheaper overall)
  • Don't trade exotics — Spreads can be 50-100+ pips
📊 Cost Comparison (100 trades on EUR/USD):
• 1 pip spread × $10 per pip × 100 trades = $1,000 in spread costs
• 0.5 pip spread × $10 × 100 trades = $500 in spread costs
Tighter spreads save you real money.

Spreads Matter More Than You Think

Over hundreds of trades, spreads add up. A 0.5 pip difference might seem small, but over 1,000 trades, it's thousands of dollars.

I only trade major pairs during peak hours with a variable spread broker. My average spread on EUR/USD is 0.1-0.3 pips. That keeps my costs low and my profits higher.

Choose a broker with tight variable spreads. Trade during peak hours. Avoid news events. Your account will thank you.
