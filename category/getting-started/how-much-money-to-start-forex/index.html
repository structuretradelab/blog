One of the most common questions I get from new traders is: "How much money do I actually need to start trading forex?"

The short answer: You can start with as little as $10-$100. But just because you can doesn't always mean you should.

In this guide, I'll give you realistic numbers for different account sizes, what you can expect from each, and my honest recommendation after 6 years of trading.

💡 Key Takeaway: The most important number isn't how much you start with — it's how much you risk per trade (1%). Account size matters less than risk management.

Minimum Deposit Requirements

Most brokers have minimum deposits. Here's what typical brokers require:

Account Type Minimum Deposit Best For
Standard / Raw Account $10 — $100 Most beginners
Standard Cent Account $10 — $50 Micro trading, testing strategies
Raw Account $50 — $500 Active traders, Scalpers
📌 Important: Don't deposit the minimum just because you can. A $10 account is extremely difficult to trade without blowing it. Start with an amount you can afford to lose entirely — but enough to trade reasonably.

Realistic Account Sizes for Beginners

$50 — $100 (Absolute Minimum)

  • What you can trade: Micro lots (0.01) only
  • Risk per trade (1%): $0.50 — $1.00
  • Reality check: Very difficult to grow. One losing streak will hurt.
  • Best use: Learning platform mechanics, testing strategies, proving you can follow rules with minimal risk.

$200 — $500 (Recommended Minimum)

  • What you can trade: Micro lots (0.01 — 0.05)
  • Risk per trade (1%): $2 — $5
  • Reality check: Enough breathing room to survive losing streaks.
  • Best use: Serious learning with enough capital to trade realistically.

$1,000 — $2,000 (Ideal for Serious Beginners)

  • What you can trade: Micro to mini lots (0.01 — 0.20)
  • Risk per trade (1%): $10 — $20
  • Reality check: Allows proper position sizing and emotional control.
  • Best use: Serious traders who want to learn with realistic stakes.

$5,000+ (Experienced Only)

  • What you can trade: Mini to standard lots
  • Reality check: Not recommended for beginners. Learn first, scale later.
⚠️ Don't Make My Mistake: I started with $5,000 because I thought "more money = more profits." I lost most of it in my first 3 months. Start small. Lose cheap. Learn. Then scale.

Why Starting Small is Smarter

Most beginners want to start with a large account because they think it will make them more money faster. Here's why that's backward:

  • You will make mistakes: Every trader makes mistakes. Better to make them with $100 than $10,000.
  • Emotions are different with real money: A $10 loss teaches you something. A $1,000 loss traumatizes you.
  • You can always add more: Start small. Prove you can be profitable. Then add funds.
  • Survival first: A small account that survives is better than a large account that blows up.
⚠️ The Smart Path: Demo account (2-3 months) → $200-500 live account → Prove profitability → Scale up. This path saves you thousands of dollars.

My Honest Recommendation

After 6 years of trading, here's the path I recommend for beginners:

  1. Start with a demo account: Trade with $10,000 virtual for 2-3 months. Prove you can be profitable.
  2. Open a small live account: Deposit $200 — $500. This is enough to trade micro lots with reasonable risk.
  3. Trade micro lots (0.01): Each pip is $0.10. A 20-pip loss costs $2. This gives you breathing room.
  4. Prove consistency for 3-6 months: If you can grow your small account steadily, you're ready to scale.
  5. Scale up gradually: Add funds in increments. Never risk more than 1% per trade, regardless of account size.
📌 My Path: I wish I had started with $200, not $5,000. I would have saved thousands in "tuition." Learn from my mistake. Start small. Lose cheap. Learn. Then scale.

Start Small, Learn Properly, Scale Later

The amount of money you start with matters less than your consistency and risk management. A $500 account with 1% risk per trade will outlast a $10,000 account with 10% risk per trade.

After 6 years, I can tell you that starting with too much money too soon is one of the biggest mistakes new traders make. You don't need to risk $5,000 to learn that trading is hard.

Start with $200-$500. Trade micro lots. Risk 1% per trade. Prove you can be profitable. Then scale. Your wallet will thank you.
