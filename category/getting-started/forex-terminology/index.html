Forex Terminology: 50 Terms Every Trader Must Know

📅 Published: May 2026 📖 12 min read 🏷️ Category: Getting Started

When I first started trading, I was overwhelmed by the jargon. Pips, lots, margin, drawdown — it felt like traders were speaking a different language. I kept googling terms in the middle of trades, which was stressful and inefficient.

To save you the same frustration, I've compiled 50 essential forex terms that every trader needs to know. Bookmark this page. Refer back to it whenever you hear a term you don't understand.

💡 How to Use This Guide: Read through once to get familiar. Then come back whenever you encounter a term you don't recognize. After 6 years, I still refer back to glossaries.

Basic Terms (A-E)

Ask Price

The price at which you buy a currency pair. Also called the "offer" price. It's always slightly higher than the bid price.

Base Currency

The first currency in a pair (e.g., EUR in EUR/USD). It represents how much of the quote currency is needed to buy one unit of the base.

Bid Price

The price at which you sell a currency pair. It's always slightly lower than the ask price.

Broker

The intermediary that connects you to the forex market. They execute your trades and provide the trading platform.

Candlestick

A type of price chart that shows the open, high, low, and close (OHLC) for a specific time period. Each "candle" represents price movement.

Commission

A fee charged by some brokers for executing trades. Some accounts have no commission but wider spreads.

Currency Pair

Two currencies traded against each other. Example: EUR/USD (Euro vs US Dollar).

Demo Account

A practice account funded with virtual money. No real risk. Essential for beginners to learn without losing money.

Drawdown

The peak-to-trough decline in your account balance. For example, if your account goes from $10,000 to $8,000, the drawdown is 20%.

Trading Terms (F-L)

Fundamental Analysis

Analyzing economic indicators, news events, and interest rates to predict currency movements.

Leverage

Borrowed capital that allows you to control a larger position with a smaller deposit. Example: 1:100 leverage means $1 controls $100.

Limit Order

An order to buy or sell at a specific price or better. Used to enter trades at better prices than the current market.

Liquid

A market with high trading volume where you can easily enter and exit positions without large price changes.

Long

Buying a currency pair with the expectation that its price will rise. Opposite of "short."

Lot

The standardized unit of trading. Standard lot = 100,000 units. Mini lot = 10,000 units. Micro lot = 1,000 units.

Lot Size

The number of units you trade. Position sizing is how you determine the appropriate lot size based on risk.

Risk & Money Management Terms (M-R)

Margin

The amount of money required to open and maintain a leveraged position. It's essentially "collateral" for your trade.

Margin Call

When your losses exceed your available margin, and the broker forces your positions to close. Avoid this at all costs.

Market Order

An order to buy or sell immediately at the current market price.

Pip

The smallest price move in forex. For most pairs, 1 pip = 0.0001. Example: EUR/USD moving from 1.1000 to 1.1001 is a 1 pip move.

Pip Value

The monetary value of one pip. For a standard lot, 1 pip is typically $10. For a micro lot, 1 pip is $0.10.

Position Sizing

Determining how many lots to trade based on your account size, risk percentage, and stop loss distance.

Quote Currency

The second currency in a pair (e.g., USD in EUR/USD). It represents how much of the quote is needed to buy one unit of the base.

Risk Management

The practice of controlling risk through position sizing, stop losses, and risk per trade limits. The most important skill in trading.

Risk Reward Ratio

The ratio of potential profit to potential loss. Example: 1:2 means risking $100 to make $200.

Advanced Terms (S-Z)

Short

Selling a currency pair with the expectation that its price will fall. Opposite of "long."

Spread

The difference between the bid and ask price. The cost of trading. Lower spreads = lower costs.

Stop Loss

An order to automatically close a trade at a predetermined price if the market moves against you. Essential for risk management.

Support

A price level where the market has historically had difficulty falling below. Often a good place to place buy orders or stop losses.

Swap

Interest earned or paid for holding a position overnight. Also called "overnight funding."

Take Profit

An order to automatically close a trade at a predetermined price when the trade becomes profitable.

Technical Analysis

Analyzing price charts, patterns, and indicators to predict future price movements.

Trading Journal

A record of every trade you take, including entry, exit, position size, emotional state, and lessons learned.

Trading Plan

Your written rules for trading, including which pairs you trade, entry/exit rules, risk limits, and daily routines.

Volatility

The degree of price movement in a market. High volatility = larger price swings. Low volatility = smaller, choppier movements.

Volume

The number of trades or contracts being executed. Higher volume = more liquidity = tighter spreads.

1% Rule

Never risk more than 1% of your account on a single trade. The golden rule of risk management.
