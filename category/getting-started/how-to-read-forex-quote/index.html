When I first opened a trading platform, I saw something like this: EUR/USD 1.1050 / 1.1052. I had no idea what it meant. What do those numbers represent? Why are there two prices?

In this guide, let's walk through how to read a forex quote — what the numbers mean, what bid and ask prices are, and how to understand the spread.

💡 Key Takeaway: A forex quote tells you how much of the quote currency (the second currency) is needed to buy one unit of the base currency (the first currency). The bid is the sell price, the ask is the buy price.

What is a Forex Quote?

A forex quote is the price of one currency expressed in terms of another currency. Since forex is always traded in pairs, every quote shows you the value of the first currency (base) in terms of the second currency (quote).

Example: EUR/USD = 1.1050

  • 1 Euro = 1.1050 US Dollars
  • If you want to buy 1 Euro, you need to pay $1.1050
  • If you want to sell 1 Euro, you receive $1.1050
📊 Simple Formula: Base Currency ÷ Quote Currency = Exchange Rate. EUR/USD = 1.1050 means 1 EUR buys 1.1050 USD.

Base Currency vs Quote Currency

Every currency pair has two parts:

Position Name Role
First Base Currency The currency you're buying or selling (the unit of measure)
Second Quote Currency The currency you're using to pay for the base (the value)

Example: EUR/USD

  • EUR is the Base Currency (the unit being measured)
  • USD is the Quote Currency (the currency expressing the value)
📌 How to Remember: The base currency is always 1 unit. The quote currency tells you how much it costs. In EUR/USD, you're asking: "How many US Dollars does 1 Euro cost?"

Bid and Ask Prices

Forex quotes have two prices — the bid and the ask. This is one of the most important concepts to understand.

Price What It Means For You
Bid The price at which you can SELL the base currency Your sell price
Ask The price at which you can BUY the base currency Your buy price

Example: EUR/USD 1.1050 / 1.1052

  • Bid: 1.1050 — You can sell 1 EUR for $1.1050
  • Ask: 1.1052 — You can buy 1 EUR for $1.1052
⚠️ Important: The bid is always lower than the ask. That difference is the spread — and it's how brokers make money.

The Spread: Your Trading Cost

The spread is the difference between the bid and ask prices. It's your cost of trading.

Spread = Ask Price - Bid Price

Example: EUR/USD 1.1050 / 1.1052

  • Bid: 1.1050
  • Ask: 1.1052
  • Spread: 0.0002 (or 2 pips)
📊 Why Spreads Matter: If you buy EUR/USD at 1.1052, you immediately lose 2 pips. To break even, the price must move 2 pips in your favor. This is why lower spreads are better.

Reading EUR/USD: A Real Example

Let's walk through a real example:

Scenario: EUR/USD is quoted at 1.1050 / 1.1052

You want to BUY EUR/USD (go long):

  • You buy at the ASK price: 1.1052
  • You pay $1.1052 for each Euro
  • If the price rises to 1.1070, your profit is 18 pips (1.1070 - 1.1052)
  • If the price falls to 1.1040, your loss is 12 pips (1.1040 - 1.1052)

You want to SELL EUR/USD (go short):

  • You sell at the BID price: 1.1050
  • You receive $1.1050 for each Euro
  • If the price falls to 1.1030, your profit is 20 pips (1.1050 - 1.1030)
  • If the price rises to 1.1060, your loss is 10 pips (1.1060 - 1.1050)
📌 Key Insight: You always buy at the ask and sell at the bid. This is why you're immediately "in the red" when you open a trade — the spread is your first cost.

Pips and Quotes

A pip is the smallest price move in forex. For most pairs, 1 pip = 0.0001 (or 4 decimal places).

Example: EUR/USD moves from 1.1050 to 1.1051 — that's a 1 pip move.

Exception: JPY pairs — For pairs involving JPY, 1 pip = 0.01 (2 decimal places).

  • USD/JPY moves from 150.00 to 150.01 — that's a 1 pip move
📊 Pro Tip: Some brokers show 5 decimal places (1.10505) — the 5th decimal is a "pipette" (1/10 of a pip). Most traders think in whole pips.

Pairs with Different Conventions

Most major pairs follow the EUR/USD convention (4 decimal places). But different pairs have different quoting conventions:

Pair Quote Convention Pip Size
EUR/USD, GBP/USD, AUD/USD 1.1050 / 1.1052 0.0001 (4 decimals)
USD/JPY 150.00 / 150.03 0.01 (2 decimals)
EUR/JPY, GBP/JPY 162.50 / 162.55 0.01 (2 decimals)
USD/CHF, USD/CAD 0.8900 / 0.8903 0.0001 (4 decimals)
⚠️ Important: Always check how many decimal places your broker shows. A 1 pip move on USD/JPY is 0.01, while on EUR/USD it's 0.0001. They're different sizes, but they're both 1 pip.

Reading Quotes is the First Step

Understanding how to read a forex quote is the first step to placing trades. Once you understand bid and ask, base and quote, and the spread, everything else in trading becomes clearer.
