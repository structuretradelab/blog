When I first started trading, I kept hearing the word "pip" and had no idea what it meant. "The pair moved 50 pips." "I lost 20 pips on that trade." It sounded like a secret language.

A pip is the smallest price move in forex. Understanding pips is essential because everything in trading — your profit, loss, risk, and position size — is measured in pips.

In this guide, I'll explain exactly what a pip is, how to calculate pip value, and why it's the foundation of position sizing and risk management.

💡 Key Takeaway: For most pairs, 1 pip = 0.0001. For a standard lot, 1 pip = $10. For a micro lot, 1 pip = $0.10.

What is a Pip?

PIP stands for Percentage in Point. It's the smallest price change in a currency pair.

For most currency pairs, a pip is the 4th decimal place: 0.0001.

Example: If EUR/USD moves from 1.1000 to 1.1001, that's a 1 pip move.

📊 The Exception — JPY Pairs: For pairs involving the Japanese Yen (USD/JPY, EUR/JPY, GBP/JPY), a pip is the 2nd decimal place: 0.01. If USD/JPY moves from 150.00 to 150.01, that's a 1 pip move.

What About Pipettes (Fractional Pips)?

Most brokers now show pipettes — a 5th decimal place (or 3rd for JPY pairs). A pipette is one-tenth of a pip.

Example: If EUR/USD shows 1.10505 and moves to 1.10515, that's a 1 pipette move (0.1 pips).

📌 Pro Tip: Don't worry about pipettes. Most traders think in whole pips. Your profit/loss will be the same. Pipettes just give more price precision.

How to Calculate Pip Value

Pip value tells you how much money you gain or lose per pip of movement. This depends on three things:

  • The currency pair you're trading
  • Your lot size
  • Your account currency

The simple formula (for USD accounts):

  • 1 standard lot = $10 per pip
  • 1 mini lot (0.1) = $1 per pip
  • 1 micro lot (0.01) = $0.10 per pip
  • 1 nano lot (0.001) = $0.01 per pip
📊 Example: If you trade 0.1 lots (1 mini lot) on EUR/USD and the price moves 20 pips, you gain or lose $20 (20 pips × $1 per pip).

Why Pips Matter for Risk Management

Understanding pips is essential for position sizing. Here's why:

  • Stop loss distance is measured in pips — "I'll put my stop loss 20 pips below entry"
  • Risk per trade determines position size — Risk $50 ÷ (20 pips × $10) = 0.25 lots
  • Profit targets are measured in pips — "I'll take profit at +40 pips"
📌 Example: You have a $1,000 account. You risk 1% ($10). Your stop loss is 20 pips. Pip value = $0.10 per micro lot. Position size = $10 ÷ (20 × $0.10) = 5 micro lots (0.05).

Pips Are Your Trading Language

Once you understand pips, everything else becomes clearer. Stop losses, profit targets, position sizing, risk management — it all comes back to pips.

I still think in pips. "I'll risk 20 pips to make 40." "My stop is 15 pips away." It's the simplest, most consistent way to measure the market.

Memorize the pip values for your lot sizes. Use them for every trade. And always calculate your pip risk before entering.
