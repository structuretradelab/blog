You know the 1% rule. You calculate position size before every trade. But there's one more layer that separates amateurs from professionals: fixed fractional position sizing.

Most beginners risk the same dollar amount on every trade. If they start with $10,000, they risk $100 per trade — even after their account grows to $12,000 or drops to $8,000. This is inefficient.

Professional traders use fixed fractional position sizing — they risk the same percentage of their current account on every trade. This method compounds winners and protects during drawdowns.

💡 Key Takeaway: Fixed fractional sizing means you risk a fixed percentage (e.g., 1%) of your CURRENT account balance, not your starting balance. This compounds growth and automatically reduces risk during losses.

What is Fixed Fractional Position Sizing?

Fixed fractional position sizing means you risk a fixed percentage of your current account balance on every trade.

Example: You risk 1% per trade.

  • Account at $10,000 → risk $100 per trade
  • Account grows to $12,000 → risk $120 per trade
  • Account drops to $8,000 → risk $80 per trade
📌 Simple Definition: Your risk amount changes as your account changes. When you win, you risk more. When you lose, you risk less. This is how professionals compound their growth.

How It Works (With Examples)

Scenario: You start with $10,000. You risk 1% per trade ($100). You win 10 trades in a row, gaining $100 each time.

Trade # Account Before Trade Risk Amount (1%) Win Amount Account After Trade

2 $10,100 $101 $101 $10,201
3 $10,201 $102 $102 $10,303
... ... ... ... ...
10 ~$11,000 ~$110 ~$110 ~$11,110
📊 Compare: Fixed dollar ($100 per trade) would yield $11,000. Fixed fractional yields ~$11,110. The difference grows over hundreds of trades.

The Compounding Effect

This is where fixed fractional shines. Because you risk a percentage of your current account, your position sizes grow as you win.

Example over 100 trades (60% win rate, 1:2 risk reward):

  • Fixed dollar ($100 risk): Account grows from $10,000 to ~$12,000
  • Fixed fractional (1% risk): Account grows from $10,000 to ~$15,000+
⚠️ The Power of Compounding: Over time, the difference between fixed dollar and fixed fractional becomes massive. This is why professionals use fixed fractional.

Drawdown Protection

Fixed fractional also protects you during losing streaks. When your account drops, your risk amount automatically decreases.

Scenario: 10 consecutive losses

  • Fixed dollar ($100 risk): Lose $1,000 total → account from $10,000 to $9,000
  • Fixed fractional (1% risk): Risk decreases each time → total loss ~$950 → account to $9,050
📌 Key Insight: Fixed fractional makes you risk-averse when you're losing and aggressive when you're winning. This is exactly what you want.

Fixed Dollar vs Fixed Fractional

Feature Fixed Dollar Fixed Fractional

During losing streak Stays the same Decreases (preserves capital)

How to Implement This Method

Step 1: Choose Your Risk Percentage

Risk 1% per trade. This is the professional standard.

Step 2: Update Your Balance Regularly

After every trading session (or weekly), update your account balance in your position size calculator.

Step 3: Calculate Before Every Trade

Always calculate your position size based on your current account balance, not your starting balance.

Step 4: Be Consistent

Don't adjust your risk percentage based on emotion. Stick to 1% whether you're winning or losing.

⚠️ My Rule: I update my position size calculator with my current balance every Monday morning. Then I risk 1% of that balance on every trade that week. This is simple and effective.

Think in Percentages, Not Dollars

Amateurs think in dollars. Professionals think in percentages. Risking the same percentage of your current account — not the same dollar amount — is how you compound your winners and protect your losers.

Risk 1% of your current balance. Update your calculator regularly. Let compounding work for you. Do this, and your account will grow exponentially over time.
