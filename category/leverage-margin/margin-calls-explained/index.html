Margin Calls Explained: How to Avoid Blowing Your Account

📅 Published: May 2026 📖 8 min read 🏷️ Category: Leverage & Margin

If there's one thing every forex trader fears, it's a margin call. It's the sound of your broker telling you that your account is empty — and your trades are being closed at the worst possible moment.

I got my first margin call within 3 months of trading. I was using high leverage, no stop loss, and one trade went against me. Within minutes, my account went from $1,000 to $0. The broker closed everything, and I couldn't do anything about it.

In this guide, I'll explain exactly what a margin call is, how it happens, and most importantly — how to avoid it forever.

💡 Key Takeaway: Margin calls happen when your account equity falls below the required margin. The best way to avoid them? Use low leverage and always use stop losses.

What is Margin in Forex?

Margin is the amount of money required to open and maintain a leveraged position. It's essentially collateral for your trade.

Example: Your broker offers 1:100 leverage. You want to trade 1 standard lot ($100,000 position). The margin requirement is 1% of $100,000 = $1,000.

That $1,000 is "locked up" while your trade is open. You can't use it for other trades.

📊 Margin Formula:
Margin = (Position Size × Contract Size) ÷ Leverage
Example: (1 lot × $100,000) ÷ 100 = $1,000 required margin

What is a Margin Call?

A margin call happens when your account equity falls below the required margin. When this happens, the broker will automatically close your open positions — usually starting with the biggest loser — to prevent your account from going negative.

⚠️ The Reality: A margin call is not a phone call from your broker. It's an automatic process. By the time you realize it's happening, your positions are already closed.

Why do brokers do this? To protect themselves (and you) from losing more money than you have in your account. Without margin calls, your losses could exceed your deposit.

How Margin Calls Work (Step by Step)

  1. You open a trade: You deposit $1,000 and open a trade requiring $500 margin. Your equity = $1,000.
  2. The trade goes against you: Your floating loss grows to $400. Equity = $600.
  3. Losses continue: Your floating loss hits $500. Equity = $500 (equals margin).
  4. Margin call triggers: Loss exceeds $500. Equity falls below margin. Broker closes your trade.
  5. Result: You lose $500, leaving $500 in your account.
📌 Important: Different brokers have different margin call levels. Some trigger at 100% margin level, some at 50%. Check your broker's terms.

Key Terms: Used Margin, Free Margin, Margin Level

TermDefinitionExample ($1,000 account, $500 margin)
Used Margin Margin locked up by open trades $500
Free Margin Equity minus Used Margin (available to open new trades) $1,000 - $500 = $500
Equity Balance + floating profit/loss If -$200 loss, Equity = $800
Margin Level (Equity ÷ Used Margin) × 100% ($800 ÷ $500) × 100% = 160%
⚠️ Critical Level: When Margin Level falls to 100% (Equity = Used Margin), a margin call occurs. Your trades will be closed automatically.

How to Avoid Margin Calls

Margin calls are 100% preventable. Follow these rules and you'll never get one:

  • Use low leverage (1:10 or 1:20): Lower leverage = lower margin requirement = more breathing room.
  • Always use stop losses: A stop loss limits your loss to a fixed amount. Without it, one bad trade can cause a margin call.
  • Risk 1-2% per trade maximum: Even with a stop loss, risking 10% means one loss puts you dangerously close to margin call.
  • Don't overtrade: Opening multiple positions uses more margin. A few small losses can trigger margin call.
  • Keep extra funds in your account: More equity = higher margin level = safer.
  • Avoid trading during major news events: Spreads widen, prices gap — margin calls happen fast.
📌 Pro Tip: Monitor your Margin Level in your trading platform. If it drops below 200%, stop opening new trades. Below 150%, consider closing losing positions manually.

Real Example: What $1,000 Account Looks Like

Let's walk through a real scenario:

Your account: $1,000 balance. You use 1:100 leverage.

You open: 0.5 lots on EUR/USD. Margin required = ($50,000 ÷ 100) = $500. Used Margin = $500. Free Margin = $500. Margin Level = 200%.

Trade goes against you: Price moves 50 pips against you. Loss = $250. Equity = $750. Free Margin = $250. Margin Level = ($750 ÷ $500) × 100% = 150%.

Trade continues against you: Price moves another 50 pips. Loss = $500 total. Equity = $500. Free Margin = $0. Margin Level = 100%.

Margin call triggers: Broker closes your trade automatically. You keep $500 in your account.

⚠️ Notice: You didn't lose your entire $1,000. You lost $500. The margin call saved the other $500. But without a stop loss, you had no control over when the margin call happened — the broker decided.

Margin Calls Are Avoidable

The traders who get margin calls are the ones who ignore risk management. They use high leverage, skip stop losses, and overtrade.

Use low leverage. Set stop losses. Risk 1-2% per trade. Monitor your margin level. Do this, and you'll never experience a margin call again.
