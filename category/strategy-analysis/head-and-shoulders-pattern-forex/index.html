The head and shoulders pattern is one of the most reliable reversal patterns in technical analysis. When it appears at the top of an uptrend, it signals that the trend is about to reverse — and a profitable move to the downside is likely.

But most beginners trade it wrong. They see the shape and jump in before the pattern confirms. Then price continues higher, and they're stopped out.

In this guide, let's walk through how to identify, trade, and set targets for the head and shoulders pattern.

💡 Key Takeaway: A head and shoulders pattern is only confirmed when price closes below the neckline. Do not enter before the breakout — the pattern can fail.

What is a Head and Shoulders Pattern?

The head and shoulders pattern is a reversal pattern that signals the end of an uptrend and the beginning of a downtrend. It has three distinct peaks:

  • Left Shoulder: Price rises to a peak, then falls back to a support level (neckline)
  • Head: Price rises higher than the left shoulder, then falls back to the neckline
  • Right Shoulder: Price rises to a level similar to the left shoulder, then falls again

The neckline is the support level connecting the lows between the shoulders. When price breaks below this neckline, the pattern is confirmed.

📊 Visual: Imagine three peaks — left (lower), middle (higher), right (lower). Connect the two valleys between them, and you have the neckline. The shape looks like a head with two shoulders.

How to Identify It

Here's what to look for:

Component What to Look For
Left Shoulder A peak, followed by a pullback to the neckline
Head A higher peak than the left shoulder, followed by a pullback to the neckline
Right Shoulder A peak similar in height to the left shoulder, followed by a decline
Neckline The support line connecting the lows between the shoulders
Confirmation A close below the neckline (bearish signal)
⚠️ Important: The right shoulder should be roughly the same height as the left shoulder. If the right shoulder is much higher, it's not a valid head and shoulders pattern. If it's much lower, the pattern may already be failing.

What It Actually Means

To understand why this pattern works, think about the psychology behind each peak:

  • Left Shoulder: Buyers push price up. Sellers step in and push it back down. The uptrend is still intact.
  • Head: Buyers push price to a new high. But sellers are getting stronger. They push price back down to the neckline. This shows that buyers are losing momentum.
  • Right Shoulder: Buyers try to push price up again, but they fail to reach the previous high. This confirms that buyers are exhausted.
  • Neckline Break: Once price breaks below the neckline, sellers are in full control. The uptrend is over.
📌 Key Insight: The head and shoulders pattern works because it shows a gradual shift in power from buyers to sellers. It's not a random shape — it's a map of market psychology.

How to Trade It (Entry, SL, TP)

Here's exactly how to trade the head and shoulders pattern:

Entry

  • Aggressive entry: Enter on the close of the candle that breaks below the neckline
  • Conservative entry: Wait for a retest of the neckline from below, then enter when price rejects it

Stop Loss

  • Place your stop loss above the right shoulder
  • Or, for a tighter stop, place it above the neckline (but be aware of retests)

Take Profit

  • Use the measured move (distance from head to neckline, projected down from the breakout)
  • Or target the next support level below

Example:

  • Head at 1.1100, neckline at 1.1050 (distance = 50 pips)
  • Breakout below neckline at 1.1045
  • Measured move target: 1.1045 − 50 pips = 1.0995
  • Stop loss: 1.1085 (above the right shoulder)
  • Risk: 40 pips
  • Reward: 50 pips (approx 1:1.25 RRR) — adjust if needed

The Measured Move: Setting Targets

The head and shoulders pattern gives you a clear target through the measured move:

Measured Move = Distance from Head to Neckline

Then project that distance down from the breakout point.

Example:

  • Head high: 1.1100
  • Neckline: 1.1050
  • Distance: 50 pips
  • Breakout point: 1.1045
  • Target: 1.1045 − 50 pips = 1.0995
📌 Pro Tip: The measured move is a guideline, not a guarantee. Use it as your initial target, but be flexible. If price reaches the measured move and shows signs of continuing, you can trail your stop and let it run.

The Inverse Head and Shoulders

The inverse head and shoulders is the same pattern, but upside down. It signals the end of a downtrend and the beginning of an uptrend.

  • Left Shoulder: A trough, followed by a rally to the neckline
  • Head: A lower trough, followed by a rally to the neckline
  • Right Shoulder: A trough similar to the left shoulder, followed by a rally
  • Confirmation: A close above the neckline (bullish signal)

How to trade it:

  • Entry: On the close above the neckline, or on the retest
  • Stop loss: Below the right shoulder
  • Take profit: Measured move (distance from head to neckline, projected up from the breakout)
📌 Pro Tip: The inverse head and shoulders is one of the most reliable bullish reversal patterns. When it appears at a key support level, it's a high-probability buy signal.

Common Mistakes

1. Entering before the neckline break

The pattern isn't confirmed until price closes below the neckline. Entering early means you're guessing. Wait for the break.

2. Ignoring the trend

The head and shoulders pattern only works after an uptrend. If the market is ranging, it's not a valid pattern.

3. Trading patterns on low timeframes

A head and shoulders on the 5-minute chart is much less reliable than one on the 4-hour or daily chart. Stick to higher timeframes.

4. Setting targets too aggressively

The measured move is a guideline. Don't expect price to travel exactly that distance. Take profits at logical levels.

5. Ignoring the right shoulder height

If the right shoulder is much higher than the left, it's not a valid pattern. If it's much lower, the pattern may already be failing.

⚠️ The Bottom Line: The head and shoulders pattern is a powerful reversal signal — but only if you wait for the neckline break and trade it at the right location. Don't chase it.

Wait for the Neckline Break

The head and shoulders pattern is one of the most reliable reversal signals — but only if you wait for the confirmation. The pattern itself is not a signal. The neckline break is.

Wait for the neckline break. Place your stop above the right shoulder. Use the measured move for your target. That's how the head and shoulders pattern becomes a valuable tool in your trading.
