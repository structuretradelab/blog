One of the most common questions I get from day traders is: What is the best moving average period?

The honest answer is: there's no single "best" period. Different periods work for different strategies, timeframes, and market conditions. But there are some periods that work well for most day traders.

In this guide, let's walk through the most effective moving average periods for day trading, how to choose the right one, and how to combine them.

💡 Key Takeaway: The 20 EMA, 50 EMA, and 200 EMA are the three most effective moving averages for day trading. Use the 200 EMA for trend direction, the 50 EMA for pullback entries, and the 20 EMA for momentum.

SMA vs EMA: Which is Better for Day Trading?

Before choosing a period, you need to choose a type:

Feature SMA (Simple) EMA (Exponential)
Response time Slower (smoother) Faster (more reactive)
Best for Longer timeframes, trend identification Shorter timeframes, day trading
Signals Fewer, more reliable More, sometimes noisy

For day trading, EMA is generally preferred. Because day traders need to react quickly to price changes, the faster response of EMA makes it more suitable.

📌 My Recommendation: Use EMA for day trading. Use SMA for higher timeframes (daily, weekly) when you want smoother trend identification. Most day traders use EMA.

The Most Common Moving Averages (9, 20, 50, 100, 200)

Here are the most widely used moving averages and what they're best for:

Period Type Best For
9 EMA Very short-term Scalping, very short-term momentum
20 EMA Short-term Day trading momentum, pullback entries
50 EMA Medium-term Day trading pullbacks, dynamic support/resistance
100 EMA Longer-term Trend confirmation on daily charts
200 EMA Long-term Major trend direction, institutional level
📊 Why These Periods? These periods are popular because they align with Fibonacci numbers and commonly observed market cycles. The 20, 50, and 200 EMA are the most widely used by professional traders.

Best Moving Average Periods for Day Trading

Here are the three periods I recommend for day traders:

1. 200 EMA — Trend Direction

Best for: Identifying the major trend on your day trading timeframe.

  • Price above 200 EMA = uptrend → look for long trades only
  • Price below 200 EMA = downtrend → look for short trades only
  • Price crossing 200 EMA = potential trend change

2. 50 EMA — Pullback Entries

Best for: Finding pullback entries in the direction of the trend.

  • In an uptrend, wait for price to pull back to the 50 EMA, then buy
  • In a downtrend, wait for price to pull back to the 50 EMA, then sell
  • The 50 EMA acts as dynamic support/resistance in strong trends

3. 20 EMA — Momentum

Best for: Confirming short-term momentum and entry timing.

  • Price above 20 EMA = short-term bullish momentum
  • Price below 20 EMA = short-term bearish momentum
  • Price bouncing off the 20 EMA in a strong trend = good entry point
📌 My Setup: I use 200 EMA (trend), 50 EMA (pullback), and 20 EMA (momentum) on the 1-hour chart. When all three align (price above all three in an uptrend), I look for long entries on pullbacks to the 20 EMA.

How to Combine Moving Averages

The real power of moving averages comes from combining them. Here's how:

The 3-EMA Setup (Most Popular for Day Trading)

  • 200 EMA: Trend direction (only trade in the direction of the 200 EMA)
  • 50 EMA: Pullback level (wait for price to reach the 50 EMA)
  • 20 EMA: Entry trigger (enter when price bounces off the 20 EMA after pulling back)

Example (Long Trade):

  1. Price is above the 200 EMA (uptrend confirmed)
  2. Price pulls back and touches the 50 EMA
  3. Price then bounces and moves above the 20 EMA
  4. Entry: On the close of the bullish candle
  5. Stop loss: Below the recent swing low or below the 50 EMA
  6. Take profit: Previous high or 1:2 risk reward
⚠️ Important: Don't use too many moving averages. Three is plenty. Five or more will give you conflicting signals and analysis paralysis.

Which Timeframe to Use

Your moving average period should match your timeframe:

Timeframe Best MA Periods Why
15-minute 20 EMA, 50 EMA Faster signals for intraday trading
1-hour 20 EMA, 50 EMA, 200 EMA Balanced — good for most day traders
4-hour 50 EMA, 200 EMA Slower signals, more reliable
Daily 50 SMA, 200 SMA Trend identification, not entry timing
📌 Pro Tip: Use multiple timeframes. Check the daily chart for trend direction, the 4-hour chart for confirmation, and the 1-hour chart for entry. This is the multiple timeframe approach.

Common Moving Average Mistakes

1. Using too many moving averages

Three is enough. Five or more creates confusion.

2. Trading every cross

Moving average crossovers happen constantly in ranging markets. Only trade them in trending markets.

3. Ignoring the trend

Moving averages work best when they align with the trend. Don't fight the trend.

4. Using the same period on every timeframe

A 200 EMA on the 1-minute chart is very different from a 200 EMA on the daily chart. Adjust your periods to your timeframe.

5. No confirmation

Don't trade moving averages alone. Use candlestick patterns or support/resistance for confirmation.

⚠️ The Bottom Line: The best moving average period is the one that fits your strategy and timeframe. Start with the 20, 50, and 200 EMA, and adjust as you gain experience.

Start Simple, Adjust Slowly

You don't need ten moving averages on your chart. Three is enough. The 20, 50, and 200 EMA will serve you well for day trading.

Use 20, 50, and 200 EMA on the 1-hour chart. Trade only in the direction of the 200 EMA. Wait for pullbacks to the 50 EMA. Enter on bounces from the 20 EMA. That's how your day trading improves dramatically.
