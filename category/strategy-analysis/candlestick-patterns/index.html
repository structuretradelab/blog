Candlestick Patterns: 10 Patterns Every Trader Should Know

📅 Published: May 2026 📖 10 min read 🏷️ Category: Strategy & Analysis

Every candlestick tells a story. It shows the battle between buyers and sellers — who won, who lost, and who's hesitating.

In my first year, I ignored candlestick patterns. I only watched line charts. I missed the signals. When I learned to read candlesticks, my entries improved dramatically.

In this guide, I'll show you the 10 most important candlestick patterns and how to use them for entries.

💡 Key Takeaway: Candlestick patterns don't work in isolation. Always use them at key support/resistance levels or trend lines.

The Anatomy of a Candlestick

Before learning patterns, understand the parts of a candle:

  • Body: The thick part — shows the range between open and close
  • Wick (shadow): The thin line — shows the high and low
  • Bullish candle: Close > Open (usually green or white)
  • Bearish candle: Close < Open (usually red or black)
📊 Key Insight: Long wicks show rejection. Long bodies show strong momentum. Short bodies show indecision.

Single Candle Patterns

1. Pin Bar (Long Wick)

What it looks like: A long wick on one side, small body on the other. The wick should be at least 2x the body.

What it means: Price was rejected strongly. If the wick is at the bottom, bulls rejected lower prices. If the wick is at the top, bears rejected higher prices.

How to trade: Enter on the close of the pin bar, in the direction of the wick. Place stop loss beyond the wick.

2. Hammer (Bullish Reversal)

What it looks like: Small body at the top, long wick below. Appears after a downtrend.

What it means: Sellers pushed price down, but buyers stepped in and pushed it back up. Potential reversal to upside.

How to trade: Enter on the close of the hammer. Stop loss below the wick.

3. Shooting Star (Bearish Reversal)

What it looks like: Small body at the bottom, long wick above. Appears after an uptrend.

What it means: Buyers pushed price up, but sellers stepped in and pushed it back down. Potential reversal to downside.

How to trade: Enter on the close of the shooting star. Stop loss above the wick.

4. Doji (Indecision)

What it looks like: Very small body (open ≈ close). Wicks can vary.

What it means: Neither buyers nor sellers controlled the period. Indecision in the market.

How to trade: Dojis alone are not trades. Wait for confirmation — a bullish candle after a doji in an uptrend, or a bearish candle after a doji in a downtrend.

Two Candle Patterns

5. Bullish Engulfing

What it looks like: A large bullish candle completely "engulfs" the previous bearish candle (body and wicks).

What it means: Strong reversal signal. Buyers overwhelmed sellers.

How to trade: Enter on the close of the engulfing candle. Stop loss below the low of the pattern.

6. Bearish Engulfing

What it looks like: A large bearish candle completely "engulfs" the previous bullish candle (body and wicks).

What it means: Strong reversal signal. Sellers overwhelmed buyers.

How to trade: Enter on the close of the engulfing candle. Stop loss above the high of the pattern.

7. Bullish Harami

What it looks like: A small bullish candle inside the body of the previous large bearish candle. The opposite of engulfing.

What it means: Potential reversal. Selling momentum is slowing; buyers are stepping in cautiously.

How to trade: Wait for confirmation — a bullish candle after the harami.

8. Bearish Harami

What it looks like: A small bearish candle inside the body of the previous large bullish candle.

What it means: Potential reversal. Buying momentum is slowing; sellers are stepping in cautiously.

How to trade: Wait for confirmation — a bearish candle after the harami.

Three Candle Patterns

9. Morning Star (Bullish Reversal)

What it looks like: A bearish candle, then a small candle (doji or small body), then a bullish candle that closes above the midpoint of the first bearish candle.

What it means: Strong reversal from downtrend to uptrend. Selling pressure, then indecision, then buying pressure.

How to trade: Enter on the close of the third (bullish) candle. Stop loss below the low of the pattern.

10. Evening Star (Bearish Reversal)

What it looks like: A bullish candle, then a small candle (doji or small body), then a bearish candle that closes below the midpoint of the first bullish candle.

What it means: Strong reversal from uptrend to downtrend. Buying pressure, then indecision, then selling pressure.

How to trade: Enter on the close of the third (bearish) candle. Stop loss above the high of the pattern.

How to Trade Candlestick Patterns

Candlestick patterns alone are not enough. Use them as confirmation at key levels:

  • At support: Look for bullish patterns (hammer, bullish engulfing, morning star)
  • At resistance: Look for bearish patterns (shooting star, bearish engulfing, evening star)
  • At trend lines: Look for patterns that confirm the bounce
  • After a pullback: Look for patterns that signal the pullback is ending
📌 Pro Tip: Higher timeframe patterns are more reliable. A pin bar on the daily chart is much stronger than a pin bar on the 5-minute chart.

Common Mistakes

  • Trading patterns without context: A hammer in the middle of nowhere means nothing. At support, it means something.
  • Ignoring the trend: Don't take reversal patterns against the strong trend. Trend is your friend.
  • Using too small timeframes: Patterns on 1-minute or 5-minute charts are mostly noise.
  • Requiring perfect patterns: Patterns don't need to be textbook perfect. Close enough is fine if the context is strong.
  • Not using stop losses: Even the best pattern fails sometimes. Always use a stop loss.
⚠️ The Most Important Rule: Candlestick patterns are confirmation tools, not standalone strategies. Always identify support/resistance or trend lines first, then use patterns to time your entry.

Learn to Read the Story of Price

Every candlestick tells a story. Once you learn to read them, you'll see the battle between buyers and sellers in real time.

Focus on pin bars and engulfing patterns at key levels. Use higher timeframes. Always use a stop loss. Do this, and your entries will improve significantly.
