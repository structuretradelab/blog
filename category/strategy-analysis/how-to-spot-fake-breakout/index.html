You've spotted the perfect breakout. Price breaks above a key resistance level with strong momentum. You enter the trade. Then price reverses instantly — and you're stopped out. You just got caught by a fake breakout.

Fake breakouts are one of the most frustrating experiences in trading. But they're not random. They follow predictable patterns — and once you know how to spot them, you can avoid them.

In this guide, let's walk through how to spot a fake breakout before it traps you.

💡 Key Takeaway: A breakout is only valid when price closes beyond the level. Intraday breaks with wicks that snap back are almost always fake. Wait for the candle to close.

What is a Fake Breakout?

A fake breakout (also called a false breakout or "fakeout") happens when price breaks through a key support or resistance level, then quickly reverses back. Traders who entered on the breakout are trapped on the wrong side of the market.

Example:

  • Resistance is at 1.1050
  • Price spikes to 1.1055 (breaks resistance)
  • Traders enter long, expecting the breakout to continue
  • Price reverses and closes at 1.1040 (back below resistance)
  • Breakout traders are stopped out
⚠️ The Hard Truth: Fake breakouts are one of the most common ways retail traders lose money. The market is designed to hunt stop losses around obvious levels.

Why Fake Breakouts Happen

Fake breakouts are not accidents. They are intentional market behavior.

1. Stop Loss Hunting

Large traders know where retail stop losses are placed — just above resistance or just below support. They push price through these levels to trigger stops, then reverse the price once they've filled their own positions.

2. Lack of Liquidity

During low-volume sessions (like the Asian session), it doesn't take much to push price through a level. There aren't enough buyers or sellers to sustain the move, so price snaps back.

3. Trapped Traders Reversing

When price breaks a level, some traders enter. When price reverses, they panic and close their positions. This panic selling (or buying) accelerates the reversal, creating even more fake breakout momentum.

4. Profit Taking

When price breaks a major level, traders who were in the trend take profits. This selling pressure can overwhelm the breakout momentum and reverse the price.

📌 Key Insight: Fake breakouts exist because markets are designed to move against the majority. When everyone sees the same breakout, the market often does the opposite.

Key Signs of a Fake Breakout

Here are the signs I look for to identify a fake breakout before entering:

Sign What It Means
Wick breaks, body doesn't close Price spiked beyond the level but closed back inside. Almost always a fake breakout.
Break happens during low volume Asian session, late Friday, holiday sessions — low liquidity equals weak breakouts.
No momentum behind the break The breakout candle is small or indecisive (doji, spinning top). Real breakouts have strong bodies.
Immediate reversal candle The candle right after the breakout is the opposite color. Shows the breakout was rejected.
Breakout happens against the higher timeframe trend Breakouts against the daily trend are much more likely to fail.
Multiple touches before the break If price has tested the level 3+ times, a break is more likely to be real. First-time breaks often fail.
Breakout right before major news News events cause unpredictable moves. Breakouts before news are almost always fake.
📌 My Rule: I never trade a breakout until the candle closes beyond the level. If price wicks above resistance but closes back below, I ignore it. The wick shows rejection — not a breakout.

How to Avoid Getting Trapped

Here's how to avoid fake breakouts:

1. Wait for the Close

The most important rule: wait for the candle to close beyond the level. A wick break is not a breakout. Only a close above resistance (or below support) confirms the break.

2. Wait for the Retest

After a breakout, wait for price to return to the broken level (retest). If the level holds as new support (for a bullish break) or resistance (for a bearish break), the breakout is confirmed. This is the highest-probability entry.

3. Check the Higher Timeframe Trend

Only trade breakouts in the direction of the higher timeframe trend. Breakouts against the trend are much more likely to fail.

4. Check the Volume

Real breakouts happen with strong volume. If the breakout happens with low volume, it's likely fake.

5. Avoid News Events

Don't trade breakouts right before or during major news. Wait 15-30 minutes after the news to see the true reaction.

6. Use a Wider Stop Loss

If you must trade a breakout before confirmation, use a wider stop loss beyond the recent swing. This gives the market room to breathe without stopping you out prematurely.

⚠️ The Best Approach: Wait for the break, then wait for the retest. Enter only when the retest holds. This single rule will eliminate most fake breakouts from your trading.

How to Trade Real Breakouts (The Retest Method)

Here's my exact process for trading breakouts:

Step 1: Identify the Level

Find a key support or resistance level that has been tested 2-3 times. The more times it's been tested, the more significant the breakout will be.

Step 2: Wait for the Close

Wait for a candle to close decisively beyond the level. Not a wick — a close.

Step 3: Wait for the Retest

After the break, wait for price to return to the broken level. This can take 1-3 candles.

Step 4: Look for Confirmation

At the retest, look for a bullish candle (for a bullish breakout) or bearish candle (for a bearish breakout). This confirms the level is now acting as support/resistance.

Step 5: Enter with Stop Loss

  • Entry: On the close of the confirmation candle
  • Stop loss: Below the retest low (for longs) or above the retest high (for shorts)
  • Take profit: Next resistance/support or measured move

Example (Bullish Breakout):

  • Resistance at 1.1050
  • Price closes at 1.1060 (breakout confirmed)
  • Price pulls back to 1.1050 (retest)
  • Bullish engulfing candle forms at the retest
  • Entry: 1.1055 (close of engulfing candle)
  • Stop loss: 1.1035 (below the retest low)
  • Take profit: 1.1100 (next resistance, 1:2 RRR)
📌 Pro Tip: The retest method has a lower trade frequency but a much higher win rate. I'd rather miss some breakouts than get trapped by fakes. Quality over quantity.

My Breakout Rules

These are my non-negotiable breakout rules:

  1. Only trade breakouts in the direction of the higher timeframe trend. No exceptions.
  2. Wait for the candle to close beyond the level. Wick breaks are ignored.
  3. Wait for the retest. I don't enter immediately on the break.
  4. Wait for a confirmation candle at the retest. No confirmation = no trade.
  5. Use a stop loss beyond the retest swing. Never below the breakout level itself.
  6. Target at least 1:2 risk reward. If the target is too close, skip the trade.
  7. Avoid news events. Don't trade breakouts within 30 minutes of major news.
⚠️ My Rule: When in doubt, don't trade. There will always be another breakout. Better to miss a trade than to get trapped in a fake one.

Wait for the Retest

Fake breakouts are one of the market's best traps. But they're also one of the easiest traps to avoid — if you're patient.

Wait for the close. Wait for the retest. Wait for confirmation. Use a stop loss. Target 1:2 minimum. That's how you stop getting trapped by fake breakouts.
