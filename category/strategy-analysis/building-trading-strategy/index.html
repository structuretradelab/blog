Building a Simple Trading Strategy: Step by Step

📅 Published: May 2026 📖 8 min read 🏷️ Category: Strategy & Analysis

"Just follow your strategy" is common advice. But what if you don't have a strategy? Or what if your "strategy" is just "buy low, sell high" with no clear rules?

In my first year, I didn't have a real strategy. I had vague ideas: "Support and resistance," "Follow the trend." But I couldn't tell you exactly when to enter or exit. My results were random because my process was random.

In this guide, I'll walk you through building a simple, rule-based trading strategy from scratch — one that you can actually follow.

💡 Key Takeaway: A strategy without rules is just wishful thinking. Every decision — entry, stop loss, take profit — must be predetermined.

What Makes a Real Trading Strategy?

A real trading strategy leaves nothing to guess. Every decision is predetermined:

  • When to enter: Specific conditions that must be met
  • Where to place stop loss: Exact price or pip distance
  • Where to place take profit: Exact price or pip distance
  • How much to risk: Fixed percentage of account
  • Which pairs to trade: Specific list, not "whatever moves"
  • When NOT to trade: Avoid news, low volatility, etc.
⚠️ The Test: If you handed your strategy to a stranger, could they execute it exactly as you would? If not, it's not a strategy — it's intuition.

Step 1: Choose Your Market and Timeframe

Market: Start with one pair. EUR/USD is the best choice for beginners — tightest spreads, most liquid, most predictable.

Timeframe: Beginners should start with higher timeframes. I recommend the 4-hour (H4) or 1-hour (H1) chart. Higher timeframes have less noise and more reliable signals.

📌 My Recommendation: Trade only EUR/USD on the 4-hour chart. That's it. One pair, one timeframe. Master this before adding complexity.

Step 2: Define Your Entry Rules

Your entry rules must be specific and objective. Here's a simple framework:

Trend Filter (Direction)

  • Uptrend: Price above 200 EMA, higher highs and higher lows
  • Downtrend: Price below 200 EMA, lower highs and lower lows
  • Only trade in the direction of the trend

Entry Signal

  • Pullback to moving average (e.g., price pulls back to 50 EMA)
  • Bounce off support/resistance
  • Candlestick confirmation (pin bar, engulfing)
📌 Example Entry Rule: "On the 4-hour chart, buy when price pulls back to the 50 EMA and forms a bullish pin bar. Sell when price pulls back to the 50 EMA and forms a bearish pin bar."

Step 3: Define Your Stop Loss Rules

Your stop loss must be predetermined. Here are two methods:

  • Fixed pip stop: 20-30 pips for EUR/USD on 4-hour chart
  • Technical stop: Below the recent swing low (for long trades) or above recent swing high (for short trades)
⚠️ Never move your stop loss further away. This rule is non-negotiable. Set it and forget it.
📖 Related Reading: Stop Loss Explained — Master stop loss placement.

Step 4: Define Your Take Profit Rules

Your take profit should give you at least a 1:2 risk-reward ratio. If your stop is 20 pips, your target should be at least 40 pips.

  • Fixed pip target: 2x or 3x your stop loss distance
  • Technical target: Previous high/low, resistance/support level
📌 My Standard: I use a 1:2 minimum. Risk 20 pips to make 40. This allows me to be wrong more than half the time and still be profitable.
📖 Related Reading: Risk Reward Ratio — Why 1:2 is the minimum.

Step 5: Define Your Risk Management Rules

Without risk management, no strategy survives. Add these rules to your plan:

  • Risk per trade: 1% of account maximum
  • Daily loss limit: Stop trading after losing 3% in a day
  • Weekly loss limit: Stop trading after losing 6% in a week
  • Maximum trades per day: 3 trades maximum
⚠️ Non-Negotiable: Risk 1% per trade. This is the golden rule. Break it and your strategy won't matter.

Sample Strategy: Moving Average Crossover

Here's a complete, simple strategy you can start testing today:

Setup

  • Pair: EUR/USD
  • Timeframe: 4-hour chart
  • Indicators: 50 EMA, 200 EMA

Entry Rules (Long)

  • 50 EMA crosses ABOVE 200 EMA (golden cross)
  • Price pulls back to touch the 50 EMA
  • Enter on the next candle close

Entry Rules (Short)

  • 50 EMA crosses BELOW 200 EMA (death cross)
  • Price pulls back to touch the 50 EMA
  • Enter on the next candle close

Stop Loss

  • 30 pips below entry (for long) or 30 pips above entry (for short)

Take Profit

  • 60 pips (1:2 risk-reward)

Risk Management

  • Risk 1% of account per trade
  • Maximum 2 trades per day
  • Stop trading after 2 consecutive losses
📌 Important: This is a sample strategy to get you started. Test it on a demo account before using real money. Adjust as needed based on your testing.

How to Backtest Your Strategy

Before risking real money, you must backtest — test your strategy on historical data.

Manual Backtesting (Simplest)

  1. Open a demo account or use TradingView
  2. Scroll back 3-6 months on your chart
  3. Move forward candle by candle
  4. Apply your entry rules at each candle
  5. Record every trade in a spreadsheet
  6. After 50-100 trades, calculate your win rate and average risk reward

What to Look For

  • Win rate: Is it above 40%? (With 1:2 risk reward, 40% is profitable)
  • Maximum consecutive losses: Can you survive the worst losing streak?
  • Average risk reward: Are you maintaining at least 1:2?
⚠️ The Rule: Never trade a new strategy with real money until you've backtested at least 50-100 trades and been profitable on demo for 2-3 months.

A Simple Strategy You Can Follow is Better Than a Perfect One You Can't

Don't overcomplicate. The sample strategy above has just a few rules. It's not perfect — but it's clear. And clear rules are easier to follow than complex systems.

Start with one pair, one timeframe, simple rules. Backtest on demo for 2-3 months. Prove profitability. Then scale slowly.
