Double tops and double bottoms are among the most reliable reversal patterns in forex trading. They mark the exact moment when a trend runs out of momentum — and a new trend is about to begin.

But most beginners trade them wrong. They see the "M" or "W" shape and jump in before the pattern confirms. Then price continues in the original direction, and they're stopped out.

In this guide, let's walk through how to identify, trade, and set targets for double tops and double bottoms.

💡 Key Takeaway: A double top or double bottom is only confirmed when price breaks the neckline (the low between the two peaks or the high between the two troughs). Do not enter before the breakout.

What is a Double Top?

A double top is a bearish reversal pattern that signals the end of an uptrend and the beginning of a downtrend. It looks like the letter "M" on a chart.

It has three parts:

  • First Top: Price rises to a high, then falls to a support level (the neckline)
  • Second Top: Price rises again to the same high (or very close), then falls back to the neckline
  • Breakout: Price breaks below the neckline, confirming the pattern

The two peaks should be at roughly the same price level. The neckline is the low between them.

📊 Visual: Imagine an "M" shape. The two peaks are the tops, and the valley between them is the neckline. When price breaks below the valley, the pattern is confirmed.

What is a Double Bottom?

A double bottom is a bullish reversal pattern that signals the end of a downtrend and the beginning of an uptrend. It looks like the letter "W" on a chart.

It has three parts:

  • First Bottom: Price falls to a low, then rallies to a resistance level (the neckline)
  • Second Bottom: Price falls again to the same low (or very close), then rallies back to the neckline
  • Breakout: Price breaks above the neckline, confirming the pattern

The two troughs should be at roughly the same price level. The neckline is the high between them.

📊 Visual: Imagine a "W" shape. The two troughs are the bottoms, and the peak between them is the neckline. When price breaks above the peak, the pattern is confirmed.

How to Identify Them Accurately

Not every "M" or "W" shape is a valid double top or double bottom. Here's what to look for:

Criteria Double Top (Bearish) Double Bottom (Bullish)
Prior Trend Must be after an uptrend Must be after a downtrend
Peaks/Troughs Two peaks at roughly the same price Two troughs at roughly the same price
Time between At least 10-15 candles At least 10-15 candles
Neckline The low between the peaks The high between the troughs
Confirmation Close below the neckline Close above the neckline
⚠️ Important: The two peaks/troughs don't need to be at the exact same price. A difference of a few pips is acceptable. But if the second peak is much higher (or lower), it's not a valid pattern.

What They Actually Mean

To understand why these patterns work, think about the psychology behind them:

Double Top Psychology

  • First top: Buyers push price to a new high. Then sellers step in and push price back down.
  • Second top: Buyers try again, but they can't push price above the previous high. This shows that buyers are exhausted.
  • Neckline break: Once price breaks below the neckline, sellers are in control. The uptrend is over.

Double Bottom Psychology

  • First bottom: Sellers push price to a new low. Then buyers step in and push price back up.
  • Second bottom: Sellers try again, but they can't push price below the previous low. This shows that sellers are exhausted.
  • Neckline break: Once price breaks above the neckline, buyers are in control. The downtrend is over.
📌 Key Insight: These patterns work because they show a shift in the balance of power. It's not the shape itself — it's what the shape represents.

How to Trade Them (Entry, SL, TP)

Here's exactly how to trade double tops and double bottoms:

Entry

  • Aggressive entry: Enter on the close of the candle that breaks the neckline
  • Conservative entry: Wait for a retest of the neckline from the other side, then enter when price rejects it

Stop Loss

  • Double top: Place your stop loss above the second top
  • Double bottom: Place your stop loss below the second bottom

Take Profit

  • Use the measured move (distance from peaks/troughs to neckline, projected from the breakout)
  • Or target the next support/resistance level

Example (Double Top):

  • First top: 1.1100
  • Second top: 1.1098
  • Neckline: 1.1050
  • Distance: 50 pips
  • Breakout below neckline at 1.1045
  • Target: 1.1045 − 50 pips = 1.0995
  • Stop loss: 1.1110 (above the second top)

The Measured Move: Setting Targets

Double tops and double bottoms give you a clear target through the measured move:

Measured Move = Distance from Peak/Trough to Neckline

Then project that distance from the breakout point.

Example (Double Top):

  • Peak high: 1.1100
  • Neckline: 1.1050
  • Distance: 50 pips
  • Breakout point: 1.1045
  • Target: 1.1045 − 50 pips = 1.0995

Example (Double Bottom):

  • Trough low: 1.1000
  • Neckline: 1.1050
  • Distance: 50 pips
  • Breakout point: 1.1055
  • Target: 1.1055 + 50 pips = 1.1105
📌 Pro Tip: The measured move is a guideline, not a guarantee. Use it as your initial target. If price reaches it and shows continuation, trail your stop and let it run.

Common Mistakes

1. Entering before the neckline break

The pattern isn't confirmed until price closes beyond the neckline. Entering early means you're guessing. Wait for the break.

2. Ignoring the prior trend

A double top is only valid after an uptrend. A double bottom is only valid after a downtrend. In a ranging market, these patterns fail constantly.

3. Trading patterns on low timeframes

A double top on the 5-minute chart is much less reliable than one on the 4-hour or daily chart. Stick to higher timeframes.

4. Setting the stop loss too tight

Placing your stop loss exactly at the second peak/trough — with no room for noise — leads to premature exits. Give it 5-10 pips of room.

5. Ignoring volume

Real breakouts happen with strong volume. If the neckline break happens with low volume, it's likely fake.

⚠️ The Bottom Line: Double tops and double bottoms are powerful reversal signals — but only if you wait for the neckline break and trade them at the right location. Don't chase them.

Wait for the Neckline Break

Double tops and double bottoms are among the most reliable reversal patterns in forex. But they only work if you wait for the confirmation. The pattern itself is not a signal. The neckline break is.

Wait for the neckline break. Place your stop beyond the second peak/trough. Use the measured move for your target. That's how double tops and double bottoms become valuable tools in your trading.
