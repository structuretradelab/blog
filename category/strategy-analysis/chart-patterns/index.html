You've learned support and resistance. You understand trend lines. Now it's time to recognize the shapes the market makes as it moves — chart patterns.

In my first year, I saw these patterns but didn't know how to trade them. A head and shoulders looked like random peaks. A double top looked like price stalling. I didn't realize they were signals of reversal.

In this guide, I'll show you the most reliable chart patterns and how to trade them.

💡 Key Takeaway: Chart patterns work because they represent human psychology — fear, greed, and indecision. Once you see them, you can't unsee them.

Why Chart Patterns Work

Chart patterns work because they reflect the psychology of market participants:

  • Fear and greed: Patterns show when buyers or sellers are exhausted
  • Memory: Traders remember past price levels and act accordingly
  • Self-fulfilling prophecy: Enough traders see the pattern and trade it, making it work
  • Institutional activity: Large players leave footprints in the chart
📌 Pro Tip: Higher timeframe patterns (4-hour, daily) are much more reliable than lower timeframe patterns.

Head and Shoulders (Reversal)

The head and shoulders is one of the most reliable reversal patterns. It signals the end of an uptrend and a move to the downside.

What it looks like (Top):

  • Left shoulder: Price rises to a peak, then falls
  • Head: Price rises higher than the left shoulder, then falls back to the same level (neckline)
  • Right shoulder: Price rises to a level below the head, then falls
  • Neckline: The support level connecting the lows between shoulders

How to trade:

  • Entry: When price breaks below the neckline
  • Stop loss: Above the right shoulder (or above neckline for aggressive entry)
  • Target: Measured move = distance from head to neckline, projected down from the break

Inverse Head and Shoulders (Bottom)

The same pattern inverted — signals the end of a downtrend and a move to the upside. Trade the breakout above the neckline.

⚠️ Don't enter before the break: Wait for price to close below the neckline (for top) or above the neckline (for bottom). Premature entries often fail.

Double Tops and Double Bottoms

Double top signals a reversal from uptrend to downtrend. Double bottom signals a reversal from downtrend to uptrend.

What it looks like:

  • Double top: Price rises to a resistance level, falls, rises again to the same level, then falls
  • Double bottom: Price falls to a support level, rises, falls again to the same level, then rises

How to trade:

  • Entry (double top): When price breaks below the low between the two tops
  • Entry (double bottom): When price breaks above the high between the two bottoms
  • Stop loss: Beyond the opposite side of the pattern
  • Target: Measured move = distance from tops to neckline, projected from break
📌 Validity: The two tops/bottoms should be roughly the same level. The time between them should be at least 2-3 weeks (on daily chart) or 10-15 candles (on lower timeframes).

Triangles (Ascending, Descending, Symmetrical)

Ascending Triangle (Bullish Continuation)

What it looks like: A flat resistance line (horizontal top) and rising support line (higher lows). Price compresses between them.

What it means: Buyers are getting stronger. Each low is higher than the last. Expect a breakout to the upside.

How to trade: Buy when price breaks above the horizontal resistance. Stop loss below the last swing low.

Descending Triangle (Bearish Continuation)

What it looks like: A flat support line (horizontal bottom) and falling resistance line (lower highs). Price compresses between them.

What it means: Sellers are getting stronger. Each high is lower than the last. Expect a breakout to the downside.

How to trade: Sell when price breaks below the horizontal support. Stop loss above the last swing high.

Symmetrical Triangle (Continuation)

What it looks like: Falling resistance line and rising support line. Both slope toward each other.

What it means: Price is compressing. Expect a breakout in the direction of the prior trend.

How to trade: Wait for breakout. Enter in the direction of the breakout. Stop loss on the opposite side of the triangle.

⚠️ Don't trade inside the triangle: Wait for the breakout. Triangles can fake out — wait for a close beyond the boundary before entering.

Flags and Pennants (Continuation)

Bull Flag

What it looks like: A sharp upward move (flagpole), then a consolidation moving sideways or slightly down (the flag).

What it means: A pause before continuing upward. Buyers are resting, not reversing.

How to trade: Buy when price breaks above the flag. Stop loss below the flag's low.

Bear Flag

What it looks like: A sharp downward move (flagpole), then a consolidation moving sideways or slightly up (the flag).

What it means: A pause before continuing downward. Sellers are resting, not reversing.

How to trade: Sell when price breaks below the flag. Stop loss above the flag's high.

📌 Pro Tip: Flags are high-probability patterns because they follow strong momentum. The stronger the flagpole, the more reliable the pattern.

How to Trade Chart Patterns

Step-by-Step Process

  1. Identify the pattern: Scan daily and 4-hour charts for recognizable patterns
  2. Draw the pattern: Mark the key levels (neckline, support, resistance)
  3. Wait for confirmation: For reversal patterns, wait for neckline break. For triangles, wait for breakout.
  4. Enter on the break: Use a limit order at the break level or enter on candle close beyond it
  5. Set stop loss: Beyond the opposite side of the pattern
  6. Set target: Measured move or next support/resistance

Risk Management Rules

  • Risk 1% of account per pattern trade
  • Ensure at least 1:2 risk-reward before entering
  • Don't force patterns — not every chart has one

Common Mistakes

  • Entering before the break: The pattern is not confirmed until price breaks the neckline or triangle boundary.
  • Trading patterns against the trend: A double top in an uptrend is less reliable than a head and shoulders.
  • Using too small timeframes: Patterns on 5-minute charts fail constantly. Use 4-hour or daily.
  • Forcing patterns: If you have to squint to see the pattern, it's not there.
  • Ignoring volume: On higher timeframes, breakout volume confirms the move.
⚠️ My Recommendation: Start with one pattern — head and shoulders or double tops. Master that pattern before adding others. Trying to learn all patterns at once leads to confusion.

Patterns Are Signposts, Not Guarantees

Chart patterns are not magical. They fail sometimes — even the perfect head and shoulders can reverse. That's why you use stop losses.

Focus on higher timeframes. Wait for confirmation. Risk 1% per trade. Use a stop loss. Do this, and chart patterns will become a valuable part of your trading.
