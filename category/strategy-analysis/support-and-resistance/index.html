Before moving averages. Before RSI. Before any indicator — there's support and resistance. It's the most basic and most important concept in technical analysis.

In my first year, I ignored support and resistance. I loaded my charts with indicators. But my trading improved dramatically when I stripped everything away and focused only on key levels.

In this guide, I'll explain what support and resistance are, how to identify them, and how to use them for entries, stop losses, and take profits.

💡 Key Takeaway: Support and resistance are self-fulfilling because traders remember past prices. Banks and institutions trade these levels — and so should you.

What is Support?

Support is a price level where buying pressure is strong enough to overcome selling pressure, causing price to bounce upward. Think of it as a "floor" under the market.

Characteristics of good support:

  • Price has bounced from this level multiple times
  • Volume was high during previous bounces
  • The level aligns with round numbers (1.1000, 1.1050)
  • It appears on higher timeframes (daily, 4-hour)
📊 Example: EUR/USD has bounced from 1.1000 three times in the past month. Each time price dropped to 1.1000, buyers stepped in. That's a support level.

What is Resistance?

Resistance is a price level where selling pressure is strong enough to overcome buying pressure, causing price to bounce downward. Think of it as a "ceiling" above the market.

Characteristics of good resistance:

  • Price has been rejected from this level multiple times
  • Volume was high during previous rejections
  • The level aligns with round numbers
  • It appears on higher timeframes
📊 Example: GBP/USD has failed to break above 1.3000 three times in the past month. Each time price approached 1.3000, sellers stepped in. That's a resistance level.

How to Draw Support and Resistance Correctly

Most beginners draw lines through exact wicks. Professionals use "zones" — areas where price has reversed multiple times.

Rules for Drawing Support

  • Look for at least 2-3 touches where price bounced
  • Draw a horizontal line through the wicks (not the bodies)
  • Use a "zone" (5-10 pips thick) rather than an exact line
  • Focus on higher timeframes (4-hour, daily, weekly)

Rules for Drawing Resistance

  • Look for at least 2-3 touches where price was rejected
  • Draw a horizontal line through the wicks
  • Use a zone (5-10 pips thick)
  • Focus on higher timeframes
⚠️ Pro Tip: Start with the daily and 4-hour charts. Support and resistance are much stronger on higher timeframes. A level on the daily chart is more significant than the same level on a 5-minute chart.

Role Reversal: Support Becomes Resistance

One of the most powerful concepts in trading: once a support level breaks, it often becomes resistance. Once a resistance level breaks, it often becomes support.

Example: EUR/USD had support at 1.1000. Price breaks down through 1.1000. Now, when price returns to 1.1000 from below, that level acts as resistance.

This happens because traders who bought at support are now trapped. When price returns, they try to sell to break even — creating selling pressure.

📌 How to trade role reversal: After a support breaks, look to sell at the broken support level (now resistance). After a resistance breaks, look to buy at the broken resistance level (now support).

How to Trade Support and Resistance

Strategy 1: Bounce (Range Trading)

Buy at support. Sell at resistance. This works in ranging markets where price bounces between levels.

  • Entry: Buy when price reaches support with bullish candle
  • Stop loss: 10-15 pips below support
  • Take profit: Next resistance level

Strategy 2: Breakout (Trend Trading)

When price breaks through a key level, it often continues in that direction.

  • Entry: Buy after price breaks above resistance (wait for retest or 20-pip confirmation)
  • Stop loss: Below the broken resistance (now support)
  • Take profit: Next resistance level or measured move

Strategy 3: Retest (The Highest Probability)

After a breakout, wait for price to retest the broken level before entering. This confirms the breakout is valid.

  • Entry: After breakout, wait for pullback to broken level
  • Stop loss: Beyond the broken level
  • Take profit: Measured move or next level
⚠️ My Preference: I prefer bounce trades at support/resistance in ranging markets. They offer tight stop losses and clear targets. Breakout trades have higher failure rates — always wait for confirmation.

Common Mistakes

  • Drawing too many levels: Your chart should have 3-5 key levels, not 20. More levels = confusion.
  • Using exact lines instead of zones: Price rarely reverses at an exact pip. Use 5-10 pip zones.
  • Ignoring higher timeframes: A 4-hour support is stronger than a 15-minute support.
  • Trading every touch: Not every touch is a reversal. Wait for confirmation (candlestick pattern, momentum).
  • Forcing levels: If a level hasn't been touched 2-3 times, it's not significant.
📌 Pro Tip: Start with the weekly chart. Draw the major levels. Then move to daily. Then 4-hour. Add only the most significant levels at each timeframe.

Simplify. Focus on Key Levels.

Most traders overload their charts with indicators. But price respects support and resistance more than any indicator.

Remove your indicators. Draw support and resistance on daily and 4-hour charts. Trade bounces at key levels. Use tight stops. Let the market come to you.
