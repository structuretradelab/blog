Building a Simple Trading Strategy: Step by Step (Expanded)

📅 Published: June 2026 📖 10 min read 🏷️ Category: Strategy & Analysis

Now it's time to expand. A complete trading strategy includes more than just entry rules — it needs exit rules, risk management, and specific conditions for every scenario.

In this guide, we'll walk through building a complete, rule-based trading strategy from scratch — one you can actually trade consistently.

💡 Key Takeaway: A complete strategy leaves nothing to guess. Every decision — entry, exit, stop loss, position size — is predetermined.

The 6 Components of a Complete Strategy

  • 1. Market and Timeframe: Which pairs? Which timeframe?
  • 2. Entry Rules: Exactly when to enter (no ambiguity)
  • 3. Stop Loss Rules: Where to place stops and when to move them
  • 4. Take Profit Rules: Where to take profits (fixed or trailing)
  • 5. Risk Management Rules: Position sizing, daily loss limits, max trades
  • 6. Trade Management Rules: What to do after entry (trailing stops, partial profits, etc.)
⚠️ The Test: If you handed your strategy to a stranger, could they execute it exactly as you would? If not, it's not complete.

Step 1: Market and Timeframe

Markets: Start with one pair. EUR/USD is best for beginners — tightest spreads, most liquid, most predictable.

Timeframe: Use the 4-hour chart for swing trading. It's slow enough to reduce noise but fast enough to provide regular setups.

📌 My Choice: EUR/USD on the 4-hour chart. One pair. One timeframe. Master this before adding more.

Step 2: Entry Rules

Your entry rules must be specific and objective. Here's a complete set:

Trend Filter

  • Identify trend using 200-period SMA on 4-hour chart
  • Price above 200 SMA = uptrend (only long trades)
  • Price below 200 SMA = downtrend (only short trades)
  • If price is chopping around 200 SMA = ranging (no trades)

Entry Signal

  • Uptrend: Price pulls back to touch the 50 SMA
  • Downtrend: Price pulls back to touch the 50 SMA
  • Confirmation: Bullish pin bar (for long) or bearish pin bar (for short) at the 50 SMA
  • Entry: On close of the pin bar candle
📌 Example Entry: "In an uptrend (price above 200 SMA), when price pulls back to touch the 50 SMA and forms a bullish pin bar, enter long on the close of that pin bar."
📖 Related Reading: Candlestick Patterns — Pin bars are our confirmation signal.

Step 3: Stop Loss Rules

Initial Stop Loss

  • For long trades: Place stop loss 5-10 pips below the low of the pin bar
  • For short trades: Place stop loss 5-10 pips above the high of the pin bar
  • Alternative: Fixed 30-pip stop loss from entry

Stop Loss Management

  • Never move stop loss further away
  • Once price moves 30 pips in your favor, move stop loss to breakeven
  • Once price moves 60 pips in your favor, move stop loss to lock in 30 pips profit
⚠️ Non-Negotiable: Never move your stop loss further away. This rule has no exceptions.
📖 Related Reading: Stop Loss Explained — Master stop loss placement before trading.

Step 4: Take Profit Rules

Initial Take Profit

  • Minimum 1:2 risk-reward ratio (e.g., risk 30 pips, target 60 pips)
  • If using technical levels: place TP at next support/resistance level on higher timeframe

Trailing Take Profit (Optional)

  • Instead of fixed TP, use a trailing stop of 30 pips
  • Let winning trades run until the trailing stop hits
📌 My Standard: I use a fixed 1:2 risk-reward for most trades. For strong trends, I use a trailing stop to let winners run.
📖 Related Reading: Risk Reward Ratio — Why 1:2 is the minimum for consistent profitability.

Step 5: Risk Management Rules

Position Sizing

  • Risk 1% of account per trade
  • Use position size calculator to determine lot size

Daily Limits

  • Maximum 2 trades per day
  • Stop trading after losing 2% in a day
  • Stop trading after 2 consecutive losses

Weekly Limits

  • Stop trading after losing 5% in a week
  • Reduce position size by 50% after 3 consecutive losses
⚠️ Non-Negotiable: Risk 1% per trade. This is the golden rule. Break it and your strategy won't matter.
📖 Related Reading: Risk Management 101 — The 1% rule is your survival guarantee.

Step 6: Trade Management Rules

What to Do After Entry

  • Set alerts for your stop loss and take profit levels
  • Don't watch every tick — check every hour or two
  • Never move stop loss further away
  • Once price reaches 30 pips profit, move stop to breakeven
  • Once price reaches 60 pips profit, move stop to lock in 30 pips

What to Do After a Loss

  • Take a 15-minute break
  • Review the trade: did you follow your plan?
  • If you followed the plan, accept the loss. If you didn't, learn from it.
  • After two losses, stop trading for the day

What to Do After a Win

  • Take a 15-minute break (avoid overconfidence)
  • Review the trade: did you follow your plan?
  • Don't increase position size — stick to 1% risk

Complete Strategy Example

Here's everything above compiled into one complete strategy:

The SMA Pullback Strategy

Market & Timeframe: EUR/USD, 4-hour chart

Trend Filter: 200 SMA on 4-hour chart. Price above = uptrend (longs only). Price below = downtrend (shorts only).

Entry: Pullback to 50 SMA + bullish/bearish pin bar confirmation. Enter on close of pin bar.

Stop Loss: 5-10 pips beyond pin bar low/high. Once 30 pips profit, move to breakeven. Once 60 pips profit, lock in 30 pips.

Take Profit: 1:2 risk-reward minimum. Or use 30-pip trailing stop in strong trends.

Risk Management: 1% risk per trade. Max 2 trades per day. Stop after 2% daily loss or 2 consecutive losses.

Trade Management: After entry, set alerts. After loss, 15-min break. After win, 15-min break. Never increase size.

📌 Next Steps: Backtest this strategy on 3 months of EUR/USD 4-hour data. Record every trade. If profitable, trade it on demo for 2 months. Then consider going live.

A Written Plan is a Traded Plan

Writing down your strategy forces clarity. Vague rules lead to vague results. Specific rules lead to consistent execution.

Write your strategy down. Be specific. Include entry rules, stop loss rules, take profit rules, and risk management. Review it before every trade. That's how you trade with consistency.
