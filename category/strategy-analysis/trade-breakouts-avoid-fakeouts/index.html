You've spotted the perfect breakout. Price surges past a key resistance level. You enter. Then price snaps back, hits your stop, and continues in the opposite direction. You just got faked out.

Fake breakouts are one of the most common ways retail traders lose money. But they're not random. They're liquidity events — designed to trigger stop losses and trap breakout traders before the market moves the other way.

In this guide, we'll look at how to trade breakouts without getting trapped. It's not about avoiding every false signal — that's impossible. It's about filtering the real ones from the fakes.

💡 Key Takeaway: A breakout is only valid when price closes decisively beyond the level with follow-through. Waiting for the retest gives you a second, higher-probability entry. If you chase the first spike, you're the liquidity .

Why Fake Breakouts Happen

Fakeouts are not accidents. They are intentional market behavior driven by liquidity.

Above resistance levels, clusters of buy stop orders accumulate. These orders belong to breakout traders looking to enter long, as well as short sellers placing stop losses. Large market participants — banks, funds, algorithms — sometimes push price briefly beyond these levels to trigger those orders, creating the liquidity they need to enter their own positions in the opposite direction.

Once those stops are filled, price reverses. From the outside, it looks like a failed breakout. In reality, it was a liquidity event .

⚠️ The Hard Truth: If you enter every breakout the moment it happens, you are the liquidity. The market needs your stop loss to fill institutional orders. Patience is not optional — it's survival.

Real Breakout vs Fake Breakout

Here's how to tell the difference before you commit:

Feature Real Breakout Fake Breakout
Candle Close Closes decisively beyond the level Wicks beyond, closes back inside
Momentum Large body, minimal rejection Small body, long wick
Follow-Through Next candle continues in breakout direction Immediate reversal candle
Prior Build-Up Tight consolidation, multiple tests of the level No prior compression — breakout from nowhere
Higher Timeframe Aligned with the dominant trend Against the higher timeframe trend
Session London/New York overlap (high liquidity) Asian session or low-liquidity windows
📌 The First Filter: If price wicks above resistance but the candle closes back below it, the breakout is fake until proven otherwise. Do not enter. Wait .

The Confirmation Checklist

Before you enter any breakout, run through this checklist. If any box is unchecked, skip the trade .

  1. Did the candle close beyond the level? (Not just a wick — a close.)
  2. Is the breakout candle strong? (Large body, minimal upper/lower wick.)
  3. Is there follow-through? (The next candle continues in the breakout direction.)
  4. Is it aligned with the higher timeframe? (Daily/4-hour trend agrees with the breakout direction.)
  5. Is there a prior build-up? (Tight consolidation, multiple tests of the level.)
  6. Are we in a high-liquidity session? (London/New York overlap, not Asian session.)
⚠️ If you answer "No" to any of these, do not trade. There will always be another breakout. Missing one trade is better than getting trapped in a fake one .

The Retest Method (Highest Probability)

Even a real breakout often pulls back to the broken level before continuing. This pullback is called a retest. It happens because the level that was resistance for weeks doesn't just disappear the moment it breaks — the market needs time to "agree" that it has flipped roles.

Why the retest is better than the breakout entry:

  • Tighter stop: You place your stop just beyond the retest zone, not the original breakout spike.
  • Confirmation: If the level holds as new support (for a bullish break), it proves the breakout was real .
  • Better risk-reward: You enter closer to the level, so your stop is smaller and your target is the same .

How to Trade the Retest

  1. Wait for the close beyond the level.
  2. Wait for the pullback to the broken level.
  3. Look for a rejection candle at the retest (pin bar, engulfing).
  4. Enter on the close of the rejection candle.
  5. Stop loss beyond the retest swing (not the original breakout point).
  6. Target the next level or measured move.
📌 The Trade-Off: Waiting for the retest means you miss breakouts that never pull back. That's fine. The trades you miss are the ones that would have trapped you anyway.

Where to Place Your Stop

Stop placement is your last line of defense against fakeouts. If you place it too tight, normal volatility takes you out. If you place it too loose, a fakeout costs you too much.

For a bullish breakout (long):

  • Place your stop below the retest low or below the consolidation range.
  • Do not place it just below the breakout candle low — that's where the market hunts.

For a bearish breakout (short):

  • Place your stop above the retest high or above the consolidation range.
  • Give it enough room to survive a wick back into the range.
⚠️ Never place your stop inside the noise. If price briefly pokes back into the range and hits your stop before continuing, you were right about the direction but wrong about the stop. Give it room.

My Breakout Rules

These are my non-negotiable rules for trading breakouts:

  1. Never enter on the first spike. Wait for the candle to close.
  2. Only trade breakouts aligned with the daily trend. No exceptions .
  3. Wait for the retest. I don't chase the initial move.
  4. Require a rejection candle at the retest. No confirmation = no trade .
  5. Place my stop beyond the retest swing, not the breakout point.
  6. Risk 1% per trade. False breakouts will happen. My job is to survive them.
  7. Avoid news events. Breakouts within 30 minutes of major news are almost always fake.
📌 My Mindset: I don't try to catch every breakout. I try to catch the right breakouts. The ones with confirmation, alignment, and a clear retest. If the setup isn't perfect, I walk away.

Be Patient, Not First

Fake breakouts are a constant feature of breakout trading. They cannot be eliminated entirely. But they can be managed — with patience, confirmation, and disciplined risk control.

Wait for the close. Wait for the retest. Wait for confirmation. Place your stop beyond the swing. Risk 1%. That's how you stop getting faked out.
