What is Price Action Trading?

Price action trading is a method of analysing financial markets by reading raw price movements on a chart — without relying on lagging indicators. It focuses on candlestick patterns, market structure, and key levels to identify high-probability trade setups.

The core belief is simple: all relevant information is already reflected in the price. You don't need to analyse economic data or wait for an indicator to catch up. The chart tells you everything you need to know.

Price action traders often trade with "naked" charts — just candlesticks and maybe a few drawing tools. No moving averages, no RSI, no MACD. Just price.

📊 Why It Works: Price action works because it reflects the psychology of market participants. Every candle tells a story about who won the battle — buyers or sellers. Reading those stories is more immediate than waiting for an indicator to confirm what already happened.

The Three Core Concepts

Price action trading is built on three foundational concepts:

1. Market Structure (Trend)

The overall direction of the market. An uptrend is defined by higher highs and higher lows. A downtrend is defined by lower highs and lower lows. A ranging market makes neither.

Your job: Identify the trend. Trade with it, not against it.

2. Key Levels (Support & Resistance)

Price levels where the market has historically reacted. These are zones where buyers or sellers have stepped in before. They act as "decision areas" where price is likely to pause or reverse.

Your job: Mark the key levels. Wait for price to reach them.

3. Candlestick Behaviour (Signals)

The shape of individual candles or groups of candles. A long wick shows rejection. A strong body shows momentum. An engulfing pattern shows a shift in power.

Your job: Wait for a signal at a key level that aligns with the trend.

📌 The Formula: Trend + Key Level + Candlestick Signal = High-Probability Trade. If any of the three is missing, there is no trade.

Key Price Action Patterns

Here are the most important price action patterns every trader should know:

Pattern Type What It Signals
Pin Bar Reversal Long wick shows rejection of a price level
Engulfing Candle Reversal A strong candle completely covers the previous one — momentum shift
Inside Bar Consolidation A smaller candle inside the previous one — pause before a breakout
Double Top/Bottom Reversal Two failed attempts at a level — trend exhaustion
Flag/Pennant Continuation A brief pause before the trend continues
⚠️ Important: A pattern without context is meaningless. A pin bar in the middle of nowhere is not a signal. A pin bar at a key support level, in the direction of the trend, is a signal.

How to Read Price Action (Step by Step)

Here's the exact process I use to read price action:

Step 1: Identify the Trend (Higher Timeframe)

Start on the daily or 4-hour chart. Is price making higher highs and higher lows (uptrend)? Lower highs and lower lows (downtrend)? Or is it ranging?

Result: You now have a bias. Uptrend = only look for longs. Downtrend = only look for shorts.

Step 2: Mark the Key Levels

Draw horizontal zones at areas where price has reacted before. These are your support and resistance levels. Use zones, not lines.

Result: You now know where price is likely to react.

Step 3: Wait for Price to Reach a Level

Don't chase price. Wait for it to come to your level. Patience is the edge .

Result: Price is now at a decision point.

Step 4: Look for a Candlestick Signal

At the level, look for a pin bar, engulfing candle, or inside bar that confirms the level is holding. The signal must align with your bias.

Result: You have a confirmed setup.

Step 5: Enter with a Stop Loss

Enter on the close of the signal candle. Place your stop loss beyond the invalidation point (below the pin bar low for a long, above the high for a short). Target at least 1:2 risk reward.

📌 My Routine: Daily chart for bias. 4-hour chart for levels. 1-hour chart for entry signals. If all three align, I take the trade. If not, I wait.

Price Action vs Indicators

Factor Price Action Indicators
Lag No lag — you read price in real-time Lagging — based on past price
Subjectivity High — different traders see different things Low — objective signals
Learning curve Steep — takes time to read charts Easy — just add the indicator
Best for Discretionary traders who want context Systematic traders who want rules
⚠️ The Truth: You don't have to choose one or the other. Many successful traders use price action for entries and a simple indicator (like a 200 EMA) for trend confirmation. The indicator doesn't lead — it just confirms what price is already saying.

Common Mistakes

1. Trading patterns in isolation

A pin bar is not a signal on its own. It must be at a key level, in the direction of the trend.

2. Ignoring the higher timeframe

A bullish signal on the 15-minute chart means nothing if the daily chart is in a strong downtrend.

3. Overtrading

Price action requires patience. If there's no setup, there's no trade. Don't force it .

4. Being too subjective

Two traders can look at the same chart and see different things. Define your rules clearly — what qualifies as a pin bar, what counts as a key level — to avoid confusion.

5. Ignoring risk management

Price action won't save you from bad risk management. Risk 1% per trade. Always.

Read the Chart, Not the Indicators

Price action trading is not magic. It's not a secret strategy. It's simply the art of reading what the market is doing — right now — without waiting for an indicator to tell you.

Strip your chart back. Identify the trend. Mark the levels. Wait for a signal. Enter with a stop loss. That's how you start reading the market like a professional.
