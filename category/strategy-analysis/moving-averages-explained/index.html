When I first started trading, my chart was covered in moving averages. The 20, 50, 100, 200. I didn't know which one to use or what they meant. I just copied what I saw online.

Moving averages are powerful tools — but only if you understand what they actually tell you. They don't predict the future. They show you the average price over time, smoothing out noise and revealing the trend.

In this guide, I'll explain the two main types of moving averages, how to set them up, and how to use them for entries and exits.

💡 Key Takeaway: Moving averages are trend-following tools. They work best in trending markets and fail in ranging markets. Know when to use them — and when not to.

What Are Moving Averages?

A moving average (MA) is an indicator that shows the average price of a currency pair over a specific number of periods. It "moves" because as new price data comes in, the oldest data drops out.

Simple example: A 20-period moving average calculates the average closing price of the last 20 candles. When a new candle closes, the oldest candle drops off, and the average updates.

📊 What a moving average tells you:
• Price above MA = Uptrend
• Price below MA = Downtrend
• MA slope up = Momentum up
• MA slope down = Momentum down

SMA vs EMA: What's the Difference?

Feature SMA (Simple Moving Average) EMA (Exponential Moving Average)
Calculation Equal weight to all periods More weight to recent prices
Response time Slower (smoother) Faster (more reactive)
Best for Longer timeframes, identifying major trend Shorter timeframes, catching moves early

Which one should you use? For beginners, start with SMA. It's simpler and smoother. Once you understand how moving averages work, you can experiment with EMA.

📌 My Preference: I use SMA on daily and 4-hour charts to identify the major trend. I use EMA on 1-hour charts for entries. But most beginners don't need both. Start with SMA.

The Most Common Moving Averages (20, 50, 100, 200)

20 Period MA (Short-term)

Roughly one month on daily charts (20 trading days). Used for short-term trend and dynamic support/resistance in active trends.

50 Period MA (Medium-term)

Roughly 2.5 months on daily charts. A popular level where institutions place orders. Often acts as strong support/resistance in a trend.

100 Period MA (Longer-term)

Roughly 5 months on daily charts. Used to confirm major trend direction. Less common than 50 and 200.

200 Period MA (Long-term)

Roughly 10 months on daily charts. The "gold standard" for defining the major trend. Price above 200 MA = long-term uptrend. Below = long-term downtrend.

⚠️ The Golden Rule: The 200-period moving average on the daily chart is the most important moving average. Many large institutions use it. Respect it.

How to Use Moving Averages for Entry and Exit

Strategy 1: Price vs MA (Directional Bias)

Rules: Only trade in the direction of the moving average. If price is above the 200 MA, only look for long trades. If price is below, only look for short trades.

Entry: Wait for pullback to the moving average, then enter in the trend direction.

Stop loss: Below the moving average (for longs) or above (for shorts).

Strategy 2: MA as Dynamic Support/Resistance

In a strong trend, price will often bounce off the moving average. Use the MA as your entry point.

  • Bullish: Buy when price pulls back to the 20 or 50 MA and shows a bullish candlestick pattern
  • Bearish: Sell when price pulls back to the 20 or 50 MA and shows a bearish candlestick pattern

Strategy 3: MA as Trailing Stop

Once you're in profit, use a moving average to trail your stop loss. This lets you stay in winning trades as long as the trend continues.

  • For long trades: Move your stop loss below the 20 or 50 MA as it rises
  • For short trades: Move your stop loss above the 20 or 50 MA as it falls

The Moving Average Crossover Strategy

This is one of the most popular trading strategies. It uses two moving averages — a faster MA and a slower MA.

Common Settings

  • Fast MA: 20 period
  • Slow MA: 50 period
  • Timeframe: 4-hour or daily

Entry Rules

  • Long (Golden Cross): When the 20 MA crosses ABOVE the 50 MA
  • Short (Death Cross): When the 20 MA crosses BELOW the 50 MA

Exit Rules

  • Exit when the fast MA crosses back below the slow MA (for longs) or above (for shorts)
  • Or use a fixed take profit (at least 1:2 risk reward)
📌 Pro Tip: Crossover strategies work best on higher timeframes (4-hour, daily). On lower timeframes, crossovers happen too frequently and generate many false signals.

When NOT to Use Moving Averages

Moving averages are trend-following tools. They fail in ranging (sideways) markets where price moves horizontally without direction.

How to identify a ranging market:

  • Moving averages are flat (not sloping up or down)
  • Price keeps crossing above and below the moving averages
  • Support and resistance levels are clear and horizontal

What to do instead: Switch to range trading strategies — buy at support, sell at resistance. Or don't trade at all.

⚠️ The Biggest Mistake: Using moving averages in ranging markets. You will get whipsawed — stopped out again and again. Learn to identify ranging markets and avoid moving averages in them.

Keep It Simple

You don't need five moving averages on your chart. Most successful traders use one or two. The 200 MA for trend direction, and the 20 or 50 MA for entries.

Add one or two moving averages to your chart. Use them to identify trend direction. Take pullback entries at the moving average. Avoid ranging markets. Do this, and you'll have a solid trend-following system.
