Volume is one of the most misunderstood concepts in forex trading. In stocks, volume tells you exactly how many shares changed hands. In forex, there's no central exchange — so what does volume actually measure?

The answer: tick volume — the number of price changes within a candle. It's not perfect, but it's the best proxy we have for market activity. And when used correctly, it's a powerful confirmation tool.

In this guide, let's walk through how to use volume in forex trading — what it means, how to read it, and how to use it to confirm breakouts and trends.

💡 Key Takeaway: Volume doesn't tell you direction. It tells you conviction. High volume confirms a move. Low volume warns that a move is weak. Use it as confirmation, not as a standalone signal.

What is Tick Volume?

In forex, brokers provide tick volume — the number of price changes within a given period. If price moves up and down 100 times in an hour, the tick volume for that hour is 100.

It's not the same as real volume. Real volume measures the number of lots or contracts traded. Tick volume just measures how many times the price changed.

But here's the key: Research has shown that tick volume has a roughly 90% correlation with actual traded volume in major forex pairs. So while it's not perfect, it's a reliable proxy.

📊 What Tick Volume Tells You: High tick volume means price is moving frequently — there's activity and participation. Low tick volume means price is quiet — there's little interest. That's the signal.

How to Add Volume to MT4/MT5

Adding volume to your chart takes 10 seconds:

  1. Open MT4 or MT5
  2. Click Insert → Indicators → Volumes → Volumes
  3. The volume histogram will appear at the bottom of your chart

You can also press Ctrl + L on your keyboard to toggle volume on and off.

What you'll see: A histogram below the price chart. Tall bars = high volume. Short bars = low volume. Green or red bars (depending on your settings) show whether the candle closed higher or lower than the previous one.

📌 Pro Tip: Add a moving average to the volume histogram (right-click the histogram → Apply to → First Indicator Data). This helps you see whether current volume is above or below average.

Volume for Trend Confirmation

Volume is a trend confirmation tool. Here's how to use it:

Price Action Volume What It Means
Price rising Volume rising Strong uptrend — buyers are committed
Price rising Volume falling Weak uptrend — warning sign, reversal possible
Price falling Volume rising Strong downtrend — sellers are committed
Price falling Volume falling Weak downtrend — selling pressure is fading
📌 The Principle: A trend needs participation to continue. If price is rising but volume is falling, fewer traders are supporting the move. The trend is running on fumes.

Volume for Breakout Confirmation

This is where volume truly shines — filtering out fake breakouts.

The rule: A valid breakout should be accompanied by above-average volume. If price breaks resistance on low volume, the breakout is suspicious.

Why it works: A real breakout happens when buyers overwhelm sellers. That requires participation. High volume shows that traders are actively buying the breakout. Low volume shows that no one cares — and the breakout is likely to fail.

How to apply it:

  • Before the breakout, note the average volume (look at the last 10-20 candles)
  • When price breaks the level, check if the breakout candle has above-average volume
  • If volume is high → breakout is likely real
  • If volume is low → breakout is likely fake
⚠️ Important: Volume is not a guarantee. A high-volume breakout can still fail. But low-volume breakouts fail far more often. Use volume as a filter, not a certainty.

Volume for Reversal Signals

Volume can also warn you about potential reversals:

1. Volume spike at the end of a trend

If a prolonged trend suddenly sees a massive volume spike, it often marks exhaustion — the last push before a reversal. The spike shows that one side has made their final move.

2. Volume divergence

If price makes a new high but volume is lower than the previous high, it's a warning sign. The move lacks conviction. A reversal may be coming.

3. Reversal pattern + volume

If you see a reversal candlestick pattern (like a pin bar) and it forms with high volume, the signal is stronger. The volume confirms that traders reacted at that level.

📌 Pro Tip: Volume divergence is more reliable on higher timeframes (4-hour, daily) than on lower timeframes. A daily volume divergence can signal a major trend change.

The Limitations of Forex Volume

Volume in forex is useful, but it's not perfect. Here's what you need to know:

  • It's broker-specific: Different brokers show different tick volumes. There's no central exchange, so the data varies.
  • It's not real volume: Tick volume measures price changes, not lots traded. It's a proxy, not the truth.
  • It's unreliable in thin markets: During low-liquidity sessions (Asian session, holidays), volume readings can be misleading.
  • It's less useful for forex than stocks: In stocks, volume is precise. In forex, it's an estimate.
⚠️ The Bottom Line: Don't rely on volume alone. Use it as one piece of the puzzle — confirmation, not a signal.

My Approach to Volume

After all these years, here's how I use volume in my trading:

  1. I always have the volume histogram on my chart. It's my first confirmation tool.
  2. I check volume before entering a breakout. If the breakout candle has low volume, I skip it.
  3. I look for volume divergence on the daily chart. If price makes a new high but volume is lower, I'm cautious about longs.
  4. I don't use volume as a standalone signal. It's always combined with price action — support/resistance, trend lines, candlestick patterns.
📌 My Rule: Volume confirms, it doesn't predict. I never enter a trade just because "volume is high." I enter because price action gives me a setup, and volume confirms it.

Volume is Confirmation, Not a Crystal Ball

Volume won't tell you where price is going. But it will tell you how much conviction is behind a move. High volume = real interest. Low volume = fading interest.

Add volume to your chart. Check it before every breakout. Look for divergence at trend extremes. But always remember: price action comes first. Volume is just confirmation.
