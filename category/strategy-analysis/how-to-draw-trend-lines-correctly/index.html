Trend lines are one of the most powerful tools in technical analysis. But most traders draw them wrong.

They draw through candle bodies. They force lines that don't exist. They draw ten lines on one chart. Then they wonder why price doesn't respect their lines.

In this guide, let's walk through how to draw trend lines like a professional — with the correct method, common mistakes to avoid, and how to trade them.

What Are Trend Lines?

A trend line is a straight line drawn on a chart that connects two or more swing points. It shows the direction of the trend and acts as dynamic support or resistance.

Uptrend line: Connects higher lows. Drawn below the price. Acts as support.

Downtrend line: Connects lower highs. Drawn above the price. Acts as resistance.

Trend lines work because they show where buyers (in an uptrend) or sellers (in a downtrend) are likely to step in again.

📊 Why They Work: Trend lines work because of human psychology. Traders see the same line on their charts. When price approaches the line, they buy (in an uptrend) or sell (in a downtrend) — creating the reaction.

The Correct Method for Drawing Trend Lines

Step 1: Identify the Swing Points

In an uptrend, look for the higher lows. In a downtrend, look for the lower highs. These are the points you'll connect.

Step 2: Draw Through the Wicks

Always draw trend lines through the wicks, not the candle bodies. The wicks show where price was rejected — where the actual high or low occurred.

Drawing through the bodies will cause you to miss the true trend line and get false signals.

Step 3: Connect at Least Two Points

Two touches create a trend line. But a trend line with only 2 touches is not confirmed. You need at least 3 touches to consider it valid.

Step 4: Keep the Angle Sustainable

A trend line with a 45-degree angle is sustainable. A trend line that's too steep (70+ degrees) will break quickly. A trend line that's too shallow (less than 30 degrees) isn't really a trend at all.

Step 5: Don't Force It

Not every chart has a clear trend line. If you have to squint to make it fit, it's not a real trend line. Walk away and find a better setup.

⚠️ The Golden Rule: If you can't draw a clear trend line with 2-3 obvious touches, the market is probably ranging. In ranging markets, trend lines don't work — use support/resistance instead.

What Makes a Trend Line Valid?

Not every trend line is tradable. Here's what separates a valid trend line from a weak one:

Factor Weak Trend Line Strong Trend Line
Number of touches 2 3+
Timeframe 15-minute Daily or 4-hour
Angle Too steep or too flat Around 45 degrees
Duration Days Weeks or months
Wick vs body Drawn through bodies Drawn through wicks
📌 The 3-Touch Rule: A trend line with 3+ touches is much more reliable than one with only 2. The more times price has respected the line, the stronger it is.

How to Trade Trend Lines

Strategy 1: Pullback to the Trend Line (Highest Probability)

In an uptrend, wait for price to pull back to the trend line, then buy. In a downtrend, wait for price to pull back to the trend line, then sell.

  • Entry: At the trend line on a pullback, with candlestick confirmation
  • Stop loss: Below the trend line (for uptrend) or above (for downtrend)
  • Take profit: Previous high (for uptrend) or previous low (for downtrend)

Strategy 2: Trend Line Breakout

When price breaks a trend line, it may signal a reversal or acceleration.

  • Entry: After price closes beyond the trend line (wait for retest)
  • Stop loss: Beyond the opposite side of the trend line
  • Take profit: Measured move or next support/resistance

Strategy 3: The Third Touch

When price approaches a trend line for the third time, it's often a high-probability trade. The 3-touch rule is my favorite way to trade trend lines.

  • Entry: At the third touch, with candlestick confirmation
  • Stop loss: A few pips beyond the trend line
  • Take profit: Next support/resistance level
📌 My Preference: I prefer pullback trades at the trend line. They offer better risk reward and higher probability than chasing breakouts.

Trend Line Breaks and Retests

When a trend line breaks, it often signals a trend change. But not every break is a real reversal.

Signs of a valid break:

  • Price closes decisively beyond the trend line (not just a wick)
  • The break happens with strong momentum
  • Price retests the broken trend line from the other side

Signs of a false break:

  • Price barely breaks the trend line with a wick, then reverses
  • No retest — the price just snaps back
  • The break happens during low-volume sessions (Asian session)
⚠️ The Rule: Wait for a close beyond the trend line before acting on a break. And always wait for a retest before entering. Most false breaks fail during the retest.

Common Mistakes

1. Drawing through candle bodies

Always draw through wicks. The wicks show where price was rejected. The bodies are just the open/close range.

2. Drawing too many trend lines

You only need 1-2 trend lines per chart. If you have 5, you're confusing yourself.

3. Forcing a trend line

If a trend line only has 2 touches and looks questionable, don't trade it. Wait for a third touch or find another setup.

4. Ignoring higher timeframes

A 1-hour uptrend might be a pullback within a daily downtrend. Always check the higher timeframe first.

5. Trading every touch

Not every touch is an entry. Wait for confirmation (candlestick pattern, momentum, volume).

6. Drawing too steep or too shallow

A 70-degree trend line will break quickly. A 20-degree trend line isn't really a trend. Stick to 30-60 degrees.

⚠️ The Biggest Mistake: Forcing a trend line when the market is ranging. In a sideways market, trend lines break constantly. Learn to identify ranging markets and switch to support/resistance strategies.

My Trend Line Process

Here's exactly how I draw and use trend lines:

  1. Start with the daily chart: I identify the primary trend (up, down, or sideways).
  2. Draw the trend line on the daily: I connect the most recent 2-3 swing points through the wicks.
  3. Move to the 4-hour chart: I refine the trend line and check for confluence.
  4. Wait for a pullback: I wait for price to return to the trend line.
  5. Look for confirmation: A candlestick pattern (pin bar, engulfing) at the trend line.
  6. Enter with a stop loss: A few pips beyond the trend line.
  7. Target the next level: The next support/resistance on the higher timeframe.
📌 My Rule: I never trade a trend line with fewer than 3 touches. Two touches create a line. Three touches confirm it. I wait for the third touch.
