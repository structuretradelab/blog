How to Read a Candlestick Chart: A Beginner's Guide

📅 Published: June 2026 📖 8 min read 🏷️ Category: Strategy & Analysis

When I first opened a trading platform, I saw a chart filled with red and green rectangles with lines sticking out. I had no idea what I was looking at.

Those rectangles are candlesticks. They are the foundation of technical analysis. Once you learn to read them, you'll see the battle between buyers and sellers on every timeframe.

In this guide, I'll teach you how to read any candlestick chart — even if you've never seen one before.

💡 Key Takeaway: Every candlestick tells a story. Who won the battle — buyers or sellers? How strongly did they win? The answers are in the candle.

The Anatomy of a Candlestick

Every candlestick has four key pieces of information:

  • Open: The price at which the period started
  • High: The highest price reached during the period
  • Low: The lowest price reached during the period
  • Close: The price at which the period ended
📊 Visual Breakdown:
• Body: The thick rectangle — shows the range between Open and Close
• Upper wick (shadow): Shows the High
• Lower wick (shadow): Shows the Low

Bullish vs Bearish Candles

Bullish Candle (Buyers Won)

  • Close is HIGHER than Open
  • Usually colored green or white
  • Buyers pushed price up

Bearish Candle (Sellers Won)

  • Close is LOWER than Open
  • Usually colored red or black
  • Sellers pushed price down
📌 Pro Tip: Your platform may use different colors (e.g., blue/orange, white/black). The key is whether close is above or below open.

What Different Candles Tell You

Candle Type Appearance What It Means
Long body, short wicks Tall rectangle, tiny lines Strong momentum. Buyers or sellers were in full control.
Short body, long wicks Small rectangle, long lines Indecision / rejection. Price tried to move but was pushed back.
Long upper wick Line on top is much longer than bottom Price rose but sellers pushed it down. Resistance found.
Long lower wick Line on bottom is much longer than top Price fell but buyers pushed it up. Support found.
⚠️ Key Insight: Long wicks show rejection. If you see a long lower wick at support, it means buyers rejected lower prices. That's often a buy signal.

Understanding Timeframes

Every candlestick represents a specific period of time. Common timeframes:

  • 1-minute (M1): Each candle = 1 minute — very noisy, not for beginners
  • 5-minute (M5): Each candle = 5 minutes — scalping
  • 15-minute (M15): Each candle = 15 minutes — day trading
  • 1-hour (H1): Each candle = 1 hour — swing trading (good for beginners)
  • 4-hour (H4): Each candle = 4 hours — swing trading (recommended)
  • Daily (D1): Each candle = 1 day — position trading (best for trend analysis)
📌 Recommendation: Start with the 4-hour and daily charts. Higher timeframes have less noise and more reliable signals.

How to Identify Trends on a Candlestick Chart

Uptrend (Bullish)

  • Series of higher highs and higher lows
  • More bullish candles (green) than bearish
  • Price stays above key moving averages

Downtrend (Bearish)

  • Series of lower highs and lower lows
  • More bearish candles (red) than bullish
  • Price stays below key moving averages

Ranging (Sideways)

  • Price moves horizontally between support and resistance
  • Both bullish and bearish candles appear regularly
  • No clear direction
⚠️ The Golden Rule: Don't trade against the trend on higher timeframes. Use candlesticks to confirm entries in the direction of the daily trend.

Common Candlestick Patterns for Beginners

Here are three simple patterns to start with:

1. Pin Bar (Rejection Candle)

  • Long wick on one side, small body on the other
  • Shows price was rejected strongly
  • Long lower wick at support = bullish pin bar → buy signal
  • Long upper wick at resistance = bearish pin bar → sell signal

2. Engulfing Pattern (Reversal)

  • A large candle completely "engulfs" the previous candle
  • Bullish engulfing: Large green candle engulfs previous red candle → buy signal
  • Bearish engulfing: Large red candle engulfs previous green candle → sell signal

3. Doji (Indecision)

  • Very small body (open ≈ close)
  • Shows that neither buyers nor sellers controlled the period
  • Often appears before reversals — wait for confirmation
📌 My Approach: I only trade pin bars and engulfing patterns at key support/resistance levels on the 4-hour chart. That's it. Simple and effective.

Every Candle Tells a Story

Learning to read candlesticks is like learning a new language. At first, it's confusing. But with practice, you'll start to "hear" what the market is saying.

Start with the 4-hour or daily chart. Identify the trend. Look for pin bars and engulfing patterns at key levels. Every candle tells a story — learn to read it.
