RSI Explained: How to Use the Relative Strength Index

📅 Published: June 2026 📖 8 min read 🏷️ Category: Strategy & Analysis

When I first discovered the Relative Strength Index (RSI), I thought it was magic. RSI below 30 meant "oversold — buy!" RSI above 70 meant "overbought — sell!" I made trades based solely on these levels. And I lost money.

RSI is not a magic buy/sell signal. It's a momentum indicator that tells you whether a pair is overextended. Used correctly, it's powerful. Used blindly, it's destructive.

In this guide, I'll explain what RSI actually measures, how to use it correctly, and the most profitable way to trade with it.

💡 Key Takeaway: RSI below 30 does NOT mean "buy." In a strong downtrend, RSI can stay below 30 for weeks. Always use RSI as confirmation, not as your primary signal.

What is RSI?

The Relative Strength Index (RSI) is a momentum oscillator that measures the speed and change of price movements. It ranges from 0 to 100.

What RSI tells you:

  • Above 70: Potentially overbought — price has risen too fast
  • Below 30: Potentially oversold — price has fallen too fast
  • 50: Neutral — equal buying and selling pressure
  • Trending up: RSI tends to stay above 50
  • Trending down: RSI tends to stay below 50
📊 RSI Formula (Simplified): RSI = 100 − (100 ÷ (1 + RS)), where RS = Average Gain ÷ Average Loss over 14 periods.

Overbought and Oversold Levels

Standard levels: 70 (overbought), 30 (oversold). But these levels are not absolute.

In a Strong Uptrend

RSI often reaches 80-85 before pulling back. RSI may stay above 50 for weeks. Buying at 70 in an uptrend might be catching a strong move, not a reversal.

In a Strong Downtrend

RSI often reaches 15-20 before bouncing. RSI may stay below 50 for weeks. Buying at 30 in a downtrend might be catching a falling knife.

⚠️ Critical Rule: Don't trade against the trend just because RSI is overbought or oversold. In a strong trend, the market can stay overbought/oversold for a long time.

RSI Divergence: The Most Powerful Signal

Divergence happens when price makes a new high/low, but RSI does not confirm it. This is one of the most reliable signals in technical analysis.

Bearish Divergence (Price makes higher high, RSI makes lower high)

What it means: Momentum is slowing even though price is still rising. A reversal may be coming.

How to trade: Look for short entries after divergence is confirmed (RSI turns down from lower high).

Bullish Divergence (Price makes lower low, RSI makes higher low)

What it means: Selling momentum is slowing even though price is still falling. A reversal may be coming.

How to trade: Look for long entries after divergence is confirmed (RSI turns up from higher low).

📌 Pro Tip: Divergence on higher timeframes (4-hour, daily) is much more reliable than on lower timeframes. A daily divergence can signal a major trend change.

How to Trade with RSI

Strategy 1: RSI + Support/Resistance (Highest Probability)

Wait for price to reach a key support or resistance level. Then check RSI for confirmation.

  • At support: Look for RSI below 30 (oversold) or bullish divergence
  • At resistance: Look for RSI above 70 (overbought) or bearish divergence

Strategy 2: RSI + Moving Average Pullback

In an uptrend (price above 200 MA), wait for price to pull back to the 50 MA. Check RSI — if it's near 30-40, it's a potential entry.

Strategy 3: Trendline Break + RSI Confirmation

When price breaks a trend line, check RSI to confirm momentum is shifting. RSI should cross above 50 (for uptrend) or below 50 (for downtrend).

⚠️ My Rule: I never take a trade based on RSI alone. I always combine it with support/resistance, trend lines, or candlestick patterns. RSI is confirmation, not a signal.

Best RSI Settings for Forex

Timeframe RSI Period Overbought/Oversold Levels Best Use
Daily/4-hour 14 70/30 Trend identification, major divergences
1-hour 14 70/30 (or 80/20) Entry timing, pullback confirmation
15-minute 9 80/20 Scalping (not recommended for beginners)
📌 My Settings: I use RSI 14 on the 4-hour and daily charts. I use 70/30 levels. I look for divergence on these timeframes. That's it. Keep it simple.

Common RSI Mistakes

  • Buying at 30 in a downtrend: RSI can stay below 30 for weeks. You'll catch falling knives.
  • Selling at 70 in an uptrend: RSI can stay above 70 for weeks. You'll miss huge moves.
  • Ignoring the trend: RSI works best when aligned with the trend, not against it.
  • Using too low timeframe: RSI on 1-minute charts is mostly noise.
  • Forcing divergences: Not every minor pullback is a divergence. Wait for clear signals.
⚠️ The #1 RSI Mistake: Using RSI as a standalone buy/sell signal. RSI is a confirmation tool. Always combine it with price action — support/resistance, trend lines, or candlestick patterns.

RSI is a Tool, Not a Crystal Ball

RSI won't tell you exactly when to buy or sell. But it will tell you when momentum is slowing, when price is overextended, and when a reversal might be coming.

Add RSI to your charts. Use it at key support/resistance. Look for divergence on higher timeframes. But always remember: price action comes first. RSI is just confirmation.
